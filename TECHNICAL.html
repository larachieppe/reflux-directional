3.1  Physical basis and governing equations

Because the endpoint is the sign of a regression of per-zone arrival time on zone height, what the physics must get right is the propagation of a local admittivity perturbation to a differential surface measurement, and its timing. What it need not get right — and in this model demonstrably does not — is any absolute impedance level. This section derives the field model solved in eit3d.CEM3D._assemble, states the two conditions that license it, separates them from a third question they are routinely confused with, and quantifies the contrast the method rests on.

Reduction from Maxwell to a scalar elliptic problem

Assume an ejωt time convention and a linear, isotropic, non-magnetic medium. The two curl equations are

∇ × H = Jc + ∂D/∂t  (1)
∇ × E = − ∂B/∂t  (2)

With Jc = σE and D = εE, the right-hand side of (1) collapses into a single constitutive term:

∇ × H = (σ + jωε) E = κ E,   κ ≡ σ + jωε  (3)

Taking the divergence of (3) and using ∇·(∇×H) ≡ 0 gives the exact statement of current continuity for the total (conduction plus displacement) current:

∇ · (κ E) = 0  (4)

Equation (4) is not yet a scalar problem. The electro-quasi-static (EQS) step is to drop ∂B/∂t in (2), which makes E irrotational and admits a single-valued scalar potential, E = −∇u. Substituting into (4):

∇ · (κ ∇u) = 0   in Ω  (5)

This is the equation the code solves. Note what survives: κ is complex, so u is complex, and the imaginary part of the coefficient is not decorative — it changes the real part of the solution, quantified below.

Boundary conditions: the complete electrode model

Electrodes are finite patches with a resistive contact layer, not point potentials. On electrode el with contact impedance zl and driven potential Ul:

u + zl κ ∂u/∂n = Ul on el  (6)
∫el κ ∂u/∂n dS = Il  (7)
κ ∂u/∂n = 0 on ∂Ω \ ∪el  (8)

with the compatibility and gauge conditions Σl Il = 0 and Σl Ul = 0 (9). The weak form is

∫Ω κ ∇u · ∇v + Σl (1/zl) ∫el (u − Ul)(v − Vl) dS = Σl Il Vl  (10)

and maps term-for-term onto _assemble: P1 stiffness K from _stiff_real, electrode mass matrices self._M[l] / zl added into K, coupling block B[:,l] = −cl/zl with cl = ∫el φi dS, diagonal Cll = areal/zl, and a bordering row of ones imposing (9) as a Lagrange multiplier rather than by pinning a node.

Two properties matter downstream. The form is linear in the coefficient, which is why _stiffness may legally write K(κ) = K(Re κ) + j·K(Im κ) and assemble two real problems (a skfem casting workaround, not an approximation). And zl enters (6) as a real boundary condition, not an idealisation: measured tetrapolar rejection over z0 ~ U(5,20) Ω·cm² is only 4.3×, a 16% residual. Earlier wording that contact impedance “largely drops out” was an overclaim and is withdrawn.

The two EQS conditions — and the third question they are confused with

The reduction (2)→(5) discards two distinct couplings, and each has its own smallness parameter.

  1. Retardation / radiation. Dropped when the domain is electrically small against the free-space wavelength, L ≪ λ0 = c/f. With L = 0.20 m (cylinder height; R = 5.5 cm), λ0 is 5996 m at 50 kHz and 2998 m at 100 kHz, so L/λ0 = 3.34×10−5 and 6.67×10−5: two orders of margin.
  2. Magnetic diffusion / induction. Dropped when the skin depth δ = √(2/ωμσ) is large against L, the inductive term being O((L/δ)²). Measured over both frequencies and all five tissues, δ/L runs 6.07 (urine, 100 kHz) to 54.3 (fat, 50 kHz); worst case (L/δ)² = 0.027, a ~3% correction. This is the binding condition, and why verify_forward.v1_quasistatic gates on δ/L > 5 and L/λ0 < 0.01.

Neither condition involves ωε/σ. That ratio answers a separate question — may permittivity be dropped from κ, reducing (5) to a real Laplace-type problem — and its answer here is no. EQS validity and the adequacy of a resistive model are independent questions; the comments at verify_forward.py:79–92 say so explicitly.

Why testing L against the in-medium wavelength double-counts

A frequent third “check” is L ≪ λmedium. It carries no independent information. The propagation constant in a lossy medium is γ = √(jωμκ); in the good-conductor limit ωε ≪ σ this becomes γ = √(jωμσ) = (1+j)/δ, so the attenuation and phase constants are equal and

λmedium = 2π/β = 2πδ  (11)

Hence L/λmedium = (1/2π)(L/δ). Demanding L/λmedium < 0.01 is exactly demanding δ/L > 15.9 — one quantity rescaled by 2π and reported twice. The stored metrics show the identity: muscle at 50 kHz has δ/L = 19.30 and L/λmedium = 0.00825 = 1/(2π×19.30). The independent retardation criterion must be taken against λ0, because the coupling EQS discards there rides on the vacuum displacement term, not on diffusion in tissue. Under the in-medium test, urine at 100 kHz (L/λmedium = 0.026) appears to fail a 0.01 threshold; that is the skin-depth margin restated, and at δ/L = 6.07 it is a ~2.7% inductive correction.

What ωε/σ = 1.40 means physically

For kidney at 100 kHz the code's admittivity gives σ = 0.111 S/m, εr = 2.80×104, and ωε/σ = 1.40: more current crosses perfused renal tissue as displacement current than as conduction current. Equivalently, the charge relaxation time τ = ε/σ = 2.23 μs is comparable to the 10 μs drive period, so charge deposited at an admittivity interface does not relax within a cycle: it persists with a lag of arctan(ωτ) = 0.95 rad (54.4°) and returns in the next half-cycle as capacitive current.

Three consequences, all load-bearing:

  • κ must stay complex. For a locally homogeneous region Z ∝ 1/κ, so Re Z ∝ σ/(σ² + (ωε)²) = (1/σ)·1/(1+r²) with r = ωε/σ. At r = 1.40 that factor is 0.338: a real-σ solve would overstate the kidney's contribution to the real part of every zone measurement roughly threefold. Muscle's factor is 0.867, fat's 0.969, urine's 1.000 — so the error is not a uniform scaling a differential estimator would cancel; it is strongly tissue-dependent and reshapes the sensitivity field.
  • The bolus rotates κ, it does not merely scale it. Replacing muscle with urine takes κ from 0.344 + j0.135 to 1.721 + j0.0004: the real part rises 1.38 S/m while the imaginary part falls 0.135, and arg κ goes 21.4° → 0.01°, with |Δκ|/|κbg| = 3.74. A resistive model gets both magnitude and phase of the perturbation wrong.
  • It is not an EQS violation. Nothing in (1)–(5) required ωε ≪ σ. r = 1.40 says the medium is a poor conductor here, not that the field is non-quasi-static.
The Re Z suppression factors are exact only for a homogeneous medium; in the heterogeneous forward problem the map from κ to Re Z is not a per-region reciprocal, so 0.338 is an indicative scale, not a measured error bar. The direct test — re-run a fixed trial set with admittivity() stripped to its real part and compare per-zone dZ and the decided direction — is cheap and has not been run.

The urine contrast: the physical basis of the whole method

Everything the device can see comes from the contrast in TISSUE. Urine at σ = 1.70 S/m against muscle 0.34, kidney 0.11, fat 0.043 and bladder wall 0.30 gives 5.0× over muscle, 15.5× over kidney, 39.5× over fat, 5.7× over bladder wall — far larger than the lesion contrasts EIT normally chases, and the reason a direction-only estimator is plausible where tomography was not.

The perturbation is nonetheless small in volume. GRADE_TABLE sets the bolus semi-axis at 0.10R (grade 1) to 0.30R (grade 5), i.e. 0.55–1.65 cm. Against πR²H = 1901 cm³, a grade 3 bolus (semi-axis 1.05 cm) occupies roughly 0.25% of the segment if isotropic. The method lives on a 0.25%-volume, 374%-admittivity perturbation whose ordering in time across zones is the entire signal — which is why the timing quantity converges under refinement (slope error 6.80 → 2.84 → 0.97 → 0.11% over 704 → 5675 nodes) while |Z| does not (11–76%, oscillating). The mechanism is visible in (10): Cll = areal/zl ties the level of Z to the realised electrode area, and electrode facets snap to mesh facets, so area jumps discontinuously with refinement. A differential timing statistic is insensitive to a common area scaling; an absolute impedance is not. No absolute impedance claim in this report is supported by this model. Consistently, StripWorld regresses on elec_z_realized (facet centroids, realised pitch 2.50–3.14 cm against a nominal 2.80) rather than requested positions.

The conductivity model is identical for every “simulated child”. draw_anatomy perturbs organ positions only; torso radius, height, the fat shell (sig[r > 0.82*R] = fat, a fixed 0.99 cm rind), organ sizes and every tissue value including urine are constants. Urine conductivity varies with hydration over roughly a factor of two in real children, and subcutaneous fat thickness is the single strongest determinant of surface-array sensitivity. Nothing here supports generalisation across habitus or urine osmolality.

Beta dispersion, and the frequency model the code actually uses

The 50 and 100 kHz operating points sit inside the β-dispersion, whose origin is Maxwell–Wagner interfacial polarisation at cell membranes. A cell membrane is a ~5 nm dielectric with specific capacitance Cm ≈ 1 μF/cm² = 10−2 F/m², in series with intra- and extracellular electrolytes. For cells of radius a the characteristic frequency is

fc = 1 / [2π Cm a (1/σi + 1/σe)]  (12)

With a = 25 μm (muscle fibre) and σi = σe = 0.5 S/m, (12) gives fc ≈ 160 kHz. Below fc membranes are effectively insulating: current is confined extracellularly, σ is low, and apparent εr is enormous (104) because each cell acts as a giant polarisable inclusion. Above fc membranes short out, σ rises and εr collapses. 50–100 kHz sits below fc on the rising flank, precisely where the dispersion is steepest — which explains the table: kidney (densely perfused, εr = 3×104) has the largest ωε/σ, urine (a cell-free electrolyte, εr = 80) essentially none.

The code does not model this with a relaxation. admittivity(name, f) is a two-parameter log-frequency interpolant:

σ(f) = σ0 [1 + 0.04 log10(f/50 kHz)],   εr(f) = εr,0 (50 kHz/f)0.10  (13)

rather than a Cole–Cole form κ(ω) = σs + jωε0[ε∞ + Σn Δεn/(1 + (jωτn)1−αn)]. Three costs, in order of seriousness:

  1. No characteristic frequency, so no causal link between σ(ω) and ε(ω). Kramers–Kronig ties them; for a single Debye, σ(ω) − σs = ω²τ [ε(ω) − ε∞]. In (13) the exponents 0.04 and 0.10 are set independently and satisfy no such relation: an interpolant over one octave, not a response function.
  2. Far too shallow. Over 50→100 kHz (13) raises σ 1.2% and lowers εr 6.7%. A single Debye at the fc ≈ 160 kHz of (12), normalised to the same 50 kHz values, gives εr falling ~21% and σ rising ~14% — roughly 3× and 12× steeper.
  3. No α, so no distribution of relaxation times — the parameter that makes real tissue spectra broader and flatter than a pure Debye. Nothing in the repository constrains it.
The 21% / 14% figures compare against one illustrative single-Debye relaxation from (12), not a fit to Gabriel or IT’IS data; they establish the order of the discrepancy, not its value. The test is to substitute a published 4-Cole–Cole parameterisation into admittivity() and re-run Studies 5 and 6 unchanged. Not done.

What that error actually costs. Less than it should. The estimator collapses the frequency axis before doing anything else: zone_series computes ser = dZ.real.mean(axis=1), an unweighted mean over FREQS = [50e3, 100e3] of the real part only. No feature in direction_features forms a ratio, difference or phase across frequency; the imaginary part reaches the decision only through its effect on Re Z inside the complex solve. A dispersion slope 3× too shallow therefore perturbs every zone's operating point almost identically and largely cancels in a mean-removed, sign-of-slope statistic. The frequency model is close to inert for the reported endpoint — a limit on the study's scope, not a licence. Add any multi-frequency discriminant (a Cole ratio separating a urine bolus from bowel gas or oedema is the obvious next specificity feature, since urine's ωε/σ < 3×10−4 makes it spectrally unique among the five tissues) and (13) becomes the dominant modelling error and must be replaced first.

What the physics licenses, and what it does not

  • Licensed. Equation (5) with complex κ and boundary conditions (6)–(9), at 50 and 100 kHz, in the R = 5.5 cm, H = 20 cm cylinder: retardation clears by two orders, induction costs at worst 2.7%. Differential and timing quantities from this model converge under refinement and support the reported direction claims.
  • Not licensed. Any absolute impedance value; any purely resistive simplification of κ; any multi-frequency spectral discriminant, which (13) is too crude to support; any statement about body habitus, fat thickness or urine osmolality, all constants here; and any anisotropy claim — muscle conductivity is markedly anisotropic, while TISSUE carries one isotropic scalar per tissue.

Standing limitations apply in full: idealised cylinder, literature tissue values as point estimates with no uncertainty propagation, an assumed SNR budget, and no phantom or patient data of any kind.

3.2  The Complete Electrode Model and its discretisation

Every number in this report is a functional of one object: the discrete forward map from an admittivity field to a set of tetrapolar transfer impedances. This section states that map, describes how eit3d.py realises it, and reports the mesh-convergence result, which does not converge in the quantity most readers would check first, for a reason that is instructive rather than fatal.

The boundary value problem

Let Ω ⊂ ℝ3 be the torso segment, ∂Ω its boundary, and E1…EL ⊂ ∂Ω the disjoint electrode patches. The admittivity σ(x) = σ'(x) + iωε(x) is complex and element-wise constant. Under the electro-quasi-static approximation (justified separately: skin-depth/L = 6–54, L/λ0 = 6.7×10−5) the potential u satisfies

(1)  ∇·(σ∇u) = 0 in Ω

(2)  u + zl σ ∂u/∂n = Ul on El, l = 1…L

(3)  σ ∂u/∂n = 0 on ∂Ω \ ⋃El

(4)  ∫El σ ∂u/∂n dS = Il

subject to charge conservation and a gauge:

(5)  Σl Il = 0,  Σl Ul = 0

These are exactly the equations in the eit3d.py module docstring. Three features distinguish this from simpler electrode models: the Robin condition (2) carries the contact impedance zl (Ω·cm2); Ul being one constant over the whole patch enforces metallic shunting; and (4) constrains the integral current, not a pointwise flux, so current density redistributes freely and piles up at electrode edges. A point model has none of these, a shunt model sets zl = 0, a gap model drops the shunt. Only the CEM represents a finite zl.

Existence and uniqueness. Work in the quotient space H = (H1(Ω) ⊕ ℂL)/ℂ, where the quotient removes the additive constant fixed by the gauge in (5). For real σ ≥ σmin > 0 and real zl ≥ zmin > 0 the associated form is bounded and coercive on H and Lax–Milgram gives a unique (u, U) for any I with ΣIl = 0 (Somersalo, Cheney & Isaacson, 1992). For the complex admittivity used here, σ' > 0 and ωε > 0 place the form's numerical range in a closed sector of the open right half-plane, so it is sectorial and the same conclusion follows. This matters more than it usually would: ωε/σ' reaches 1.40 for kidney and 0.39 for muscle at 100 kHz, so the imaginary part is not a perturbation on the real one.

The sectoriality argument above is stated, not verified in code. Nothing in eit3d.py checks that Re σ > 0 element-wise or that zl is passed with positive real part; a sign error in an admittivity table would produce a non-sectorial form and a silently wrong solve, not an exception. A three-line assertion in CEM3D._assemble would close this.

Weak form: electrode potentials as extra unknowns

Multiply (1) by a test function v, integrate by parts, and substitute the flux from (2) and (4). Writing V = (V1…VL) for the electrode-potential test vector, the variational problem is: find (u, U) ∈ H such that for all (v, V) ∈ H

(6)  ∫Ω σ ∇u · ∇v dx + Σl (1/zl) ∫El (u − Ul)(v − Vl) dS = Σl Il Vl

The right-hand side depends only on V, never on v: the electrode potentials are primary unknowns, coupled to the field through boundary mass terms, and the injected current loads those unknowns alone. Expanding the product in (6) gives four terms (a field–field boundary mass, two field–electrode couplings of opposite sign, an electrode–electrode diagonal), and those are precisely the four blocks assembled in the code.

The discretisation in eit3d.py

The field uses continuous P1 tetrahedra (Basis(mesh, ElementTetP1())) and the admittivity uses P0 (self.p0 = self.basis.with_element(ElementTetP0())), i.e. one constant per tet. P0 admittivity is the right pairing here because the bolus is painted onto elements by add_bolus; a continuous σ would smear the lumen boundary the model exists to track. In CEM3D.__init__, for each electrode a FacetBasis restricted to that electrode's facets yields

  • M[l] = ∫El φiφj dS, the boundary mass matrix,
  • c[l] = ∫El φi dS, the coupling vector,
  • area[l] = c[l].sum() = ∫El dS, the realised electrode area.

That last identity, area being exactly the sum of the coupling vector, is the hinge of the convergence result below. _assemble then builds

(7)  A = [[K + Σl Ml/zl,  B,  0], [BT,  C,  1], [0,  1T,  0]],   B:,l = −cl/zl,  C = diag(areal/zl)

with K the σ-weighted stiffness and the final row/column a Lagrange multiplier enforcing ΣUl = 0. The system dimension is N + L + 1; at the finest verification mesh that is 5675 + 16 + 1 = 5692. Two implementation details would silently corrupt a complex-valued solve if mishandled:

  • _stiffness assembles the real and imaginary parts of σ in separate real assemblies and recombines as K_re + 1j*K_im, with the comment that this "avoids skfem casting away the imag part". A single complex assembly would have discarded Im σ, and given ωε/σ' ≈ 1.4 for kidney that would not have been a small error.
  • The coupling block is transposed, B.T, not conjugate-transposed. For a complex admittivity this is the correct choice: (6) is a bilinear, not sesquilinear, form, so A is complex symmetric rather than Hermitian. Using B.conj().T would break reciprocity, which is exactly what the V2 check is for.

The matrix is independent of the drive pattern, so solve_drives performs one splu factorisation per frame and back-substitutes once per zone; measure_zones in strip3d.py then reads Z = (Up − Uq)/I off the inner sense pair. At 18 zones that is an 18× saving, and it is what makes 2160-trial studies affordable.

Mesh. make_cylinder extrudes a disk point set (_disk_points: rings at r = R·i/n_rings with m = max(6, round(2πr·n_rings/R)) points each) over nz equally spaced z-planes and tetrahedralises with Delaunay(..., "Qt Qbb Qc Qz"), keeping tets of volume > 10−9R2h. The docstring's convex-hull argument is sound: the disk is convex, so the hull of the extruded cloud is the polygonal cylinder exactly, with no prism-splitting bookkeeping.

Element quality is never measured. A Delaunay triangulation of a structured extruded lattice is riddled with cospherical degeneracies, and the only quality control is a volume floor. No minimum dihedral angle or radius-edge ratio is reported anywhere in the repo, so the conditioning of K is uncharacterised. Test: print the dihedral-angle histogram and κ(A) per mesh level, or re-run V3 on a TetGen mesh with a radius-edge bound and check the slope column reproduces.

Discrepancy. make_cylinder's defaults are height = 14.0, n_rings = 7, nz = 17; the production geometry (R = 5.5, height = 20.0, n_rings = 6, nz = 17) is passed explicitly at every call site. Those defaults are dead parameters and should be aligned or removed, so no future caller silently gets a 14 cm torso.

Why: electrode area snaps to mesh facets

place_strips selects an electrode as the set of whole boundary facets whose centroids fall inside an angular window |Δφ| < arc_half = 0.30 rad and an axial window |z − zk| < 0.40·dz. With N = 8 over a 16 cm span, dz = 2.286 cm and the axial window is 1.829 cm tall; the arc window is 0.60 rad, i.e. 3.3 cm at R = 5.5. The nominal patch is therefore ≈6.0 cm2 at every refinement level. The realised patch is not, because facets are indivisible:

Since C = diag(areal/zl) and B:,l = −cl/zl both scale with the realised patch, and since the sensed voltage is an area-weighted average of u over the sense patch so that a wider patch averages a smaller difference, |Z| inherits the jumps.

A candidate alternative explanation, that the polygonal cylinder's lateral area changes with n_rings, is quantitatively too small: the inscribed m-gon perimeter ratio m·sin(π/m)/π runs 0.9974, 0.9983, 0.9989, 0.9991, 0.9993 across the five meshes, a 0.2% effect and three orders below the observed 76% excursion. Facet snapping is the mechanism.

The two ratio columns above are geometric arithmetic from the selection rule in place_strips, not a measured facet census. CEM3D already stores the ground truth in self.area; the direct test is to log solver.area (all 16 electrodes) at each level of v3_mesh_convergence and check that the per-electrode area spread tracks the |Z| oscillation. This has not been done and should be.

Why the slope survives, and why this independently justifies fractional normalisation

Model the discretisation error at zone j as a real positive multiplicative gain plus a small residual:

(8)  Zjh(t) = gj Zj(t) + εj(t),   gj > 0 set by realised electrode area

The pipeline forms Zref,j as the mean of the first two frames and then

(9)  ΔZj(t) = (Zjh(t) − Zref,j)/|Zref,j| = (gjZj − gjZ̄j)/(gj|Z̄j|) = (Zj − Z̄j)/|Z̄j|

gj cancels exactly, to all orders, appearing identically in numerator and denominator. Downstream, zone_series takes Re ΔZ averaged over frequency, _strip_aperture_stats removes the across-zone and per-zone temporal means, xcorr_lag normalises each series again before correlating, and the slope is a least-squares fit of arrival time against zone_z. None of these can see gj once (9) has removed it. What remains in the slope is genuine field-resolution error, and that is the column converging monotonically, 6.80 → 0.11 percent.

The ordering is load-bearing and easy to get wrong. The common-mode step subtracts the mean across zones. Per-zone gains do not commute with that operation: had the pipeline correlated raw Zjh rather than ΔZj, subtracting a gain-weighted across-zone mean would have injected a zone-dependent, mesh-dependent component into every series and hence into the arrival times. The fractional normalisation must precede common-mode rejection, and in the code it does.

This connects to a separate audit finding and sharpens it. The subtraction of the resting reference is a provable no-op for the direction call: adding an arbitrary per-zone complex constant to ΔZ moves the decision evidence by 8.9×10−16, because every path terminates in a mean removal. The division by |Zref,j| is not a no-op; it is precisely the operation that cancels gj. So in the expression (Z − Zref)/|Zref|, the numerator's reference is inert for direction while the denominator's is doing the entire job of making the estimator mesh-independent. The normalisation was adopted for signal-scaling reasons; the convergence study is independent post-hoc justification for it on numerical grounds.

What the model may and may not claim

The conclusion is narrow and should be stated without softening:

  • Supported: fractional and differential quantities (ΔZ), per-zone arrival times, the fitted slope, its sign, the wave-linearity statistic, and the decision built from them. These are invariant to the per-electrode gain that refinement fails to pin down.
  • Not supported: any absolute impedance number. No claim of the form "the bolus produces X mΩ", "the instrument needs Y Ω of dynamic range", or any threshold in ohms is licensed here. The mean |Z| ≈ 0.3 Ω above is a solver diagnostic, not a prediction. Consequently the SNR budget must be, and is, expressed fractionally.
  • Not supported: generalisation across body habitus. draw_anatomy varies organ positions only; torso radius, height, fat thickness, organ sizes and every tissue admittivity are identical across all simulated children.
V3 establishes mesh-invariance of the decision on one trial (one label, grade 4, one side, one seed), noiseless. That is n = 1 against meshes, not a distribution. It does not exclude the possibility that near-threshold trials, which is where the unexplained 11.1% false-retrograde floor at zero motion lives, flip between meshes. Test: re-run a stratified subset of the Study 6 configuration (say 200 trials spanning grades and motion amplitudes) at the 6×17 and 8×25 levels and report per-trial decision agreement, not just the aggregate rate.

Why CEM rather than a point-electrode model

Contact impedance is the dominant nuisance parameter in surface bioimpedance, and V4 measures how well tetrapolar sensing suppresses it here: over the modelled range z0 ~ U(5, 20) Ω·cm2, the tetrapolar transfer impedance varies by 16.2% peak-to-peak against 70.3% for a two-electrode proxy on identical electrodes and identical solves. That is a rejection factor of 4.34×. Over the wider sweep z0 ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20, 40} it is 48.1% vs 164%, a factor of 3.41×. Rejection, not immunity.

A model without zl cannot produce that number at all: it reports zero contact sensitivity by construction, and the 16% residual is invisible. Since 16% is the same order as the ΔZ excursions the estimator works with, a model that hides it is unfit for purpose. That is the argument for paying the CEM's price: L extra unknowns, an L-wide coupling block, and a Robin term assembled per electrode.

Discrepancy with the code. The strip3d.py module docstring still reads "contact impedance largely rejected". Given 4.34×, that wording is the same overclaim V4 was written to retire and should be edited to state the measured factor.

Reciprocity: necessary, not sufficient

The V2 check finds a maximum relative reciprocity error of 4.77×10−15 (median 9.35×10−16) over 12 zones. The sensing section treats reciprocity physically; the point here is numerical and deflationary. The assembled matrix (7) is complex symmetric by construction (B.T, a diagonal C, a symmetric gauge border), so Z(drive AB, sense CD) = Z(drive CD, sense AB) is an algebraic identity for an exact solve, and the residual is essentially the sparse LU's. The check still earns its place: it catches a transposed or conjugated coupling block, non-symmetric facet quadrature, an inconsistent 1/zl between B and C, or a broken gauge row. But any error entering symmetrically (a wrong electrode area, a wrong tissue value, a mis-meshed geometry) passes it untouched. It is a necessary condition on the implementation, not evidence that the physics is right.

3.3  Reciprocity, lead fields, and the sensitivity of a flank tetrapolar array

Everything this device claims to measure is a differential quantity: not the impedance of a flank, but the way a small conductive perturbation reweights the transfer impedance of one tetrapolar zone relative to its neighbours, and the order in which it does so. That makes the sensitivity distribution, meaning where in the body a zone actually looks, with what sign, and how fast that falls off with depth, the single most load-bearing piece of physics in the project. This section derives it, computes it for the array geometry the code actually builds, and states plainly which of the resulting numbers are supported by the simulation and which are analytic estimates that the simulation has never been asked to confirm.

Three things must be said at the outset, because they change what the rest of the section is allowed to conclude.

  1. An earlier draft of this section was built on the wrong aperture. It quoted depth reach, bolus excursion and noise margin for the widest tetrapolar aperture, G = 7. At the array size the studies actually run, G = 7 contributes nothing to the decision: it yields one zone per strip, and _strip_aperture_stats returns None for any aperture with fewer than two zones. Every reach number below has been recomputed for G = 5, the deepest aperture the estimator can use. See §3.
  2. The estimator does not read the raw zone sensitivity. It reads the across-zone mean-subtracted series. That projection is not a small correction: it removes roughly two thirds of the excursion at G = 5 and it guarantees sign reversal between inner and outer zones at the target depth, with no shallow-lobe or motion argument required. See §6.
  3. The estimator is not sign-agnostic. An earlier draft asserted that it "uses only timing, never sign or amplitude". Both halves of that are false against the code. See §6.

1. The discrete forward operator, and what reciprocity does and does not prove

The forward problem solved in eit3d.CEM3D is the electro-quasi-static (EQS) boundary-value problem with the Complete Electrode Model of Somersalo, Cheney and Isaacson. In the interior Ω,

(1)  ∇·(κ ∇u) = 0,    κ(x,ω) = σ(x) + jωε(x)

with, on each electrode El of contact impedance zl,

(2)  u + zl κ ∂u/∂n = Ul  on El;   κ ∂u/∂n = 0 off the electrodes;   ∫El κ ∂u/∂n dS = Il

and the gauge Σl Ul = 0. The complex admittivity is not decoration. V1 measured ωε/σ = 1.40 for kidney and 0.39 for muscle at 100 kHz: displacement current exceeds conduction current in perfused tissue at the top of the band, so a real-conductivity model would be quantitatively wrong. EQS validity itself rests on the skin-depth condition (δ/L = 6 to 54) and on the domain being electrically small against the free-space wavelength (L/λ0 = 6.7×10−5); testing L against the in-medium wavelength would merely restate the skin-depth test, since λ = 2πδ in a good conductor.

_assemble discretises (1) and (2) with P1 tetrahedra into the augmented system

(3)   A = [[K + Σl Ml/zl,  B,  0], [BT,  C,  1], [0,  1T,  0]],   B:,l = −cl/zl,   Cll = |El|/zl

where Ml is the electrode boundary mass matrix, cl the electrode load vector (cl,i = ∫El φi dS), |El| = cl·1 the realised electrode area (stored as self.area), and the final row and column implement the gauge through a Lagrange multiplier γ.

Reciprocity now follows structurally. A is complex symmetric by construction: K and every Ml are symmetric, B is inserted once as B and once as B.T from the same array, C is diagonal, and the gauge column and row are the same vector of ones. Since A = AT, the discrete transfer impedance Z(ab),(pq) = (ep−eq)T A−1(ea−eb) is symmetric under exchange of the drive and sense pairs. V2 measured that exchange over 12 tetrapolar zones and found a maximum relative error of 4.77×10−15.

Do not oversell V2. Because A is exactly symmetric as coded, reciprocity is an algebraic identity for an exact solve, and 4.77×10−15 is essentially the condition-number-scaled residual of the sparse LU. V2 is a real check: it would immediately catch a transposed or mis-signed B, a non-symmetric quadrature on Ml, an inconsistent 1/zl between B and C, or a broken gauge row, and it confirms the direct solver is numerically clean. It is not an assumption-free validation of the physics. An error that enters symmetrically, such as a wrong Robin coefficient applied consistently to both blocks, a wrong electrode area, a mis-meshed geometry or a wrong tissue value, passes V2 untouched. Reciprocity is necessary, not sufficient. The project has no phantom and no analytic-solution benchmark, which is what would actually close this gap. A sphere or layered-half-space case with a closed form is the cheapest way to get one, and it has not been run. The report's phrase "assumption-free validation of the discretised forward operator" should be narrowed to "assumption-free validation of the symmetry of the discretised forward operator and of the solve".

γ is worth one more line, because it carries the contact-impedance argument in §4. Test the top block row with the constant vector 1: K1 = 0 and Ml1 = cl, so 1T(top) gives Σl[(cl·u) − |El|Ul]/zl = 0. Summing the electrode rows gives the same expression with opposite sign plus Lγ = Σl Il. Every drive built in solve_drives sets rhs[N+a] = +amp and rhs[N+b] = −amp, so ΣIl = 0 and γ ≡ 0. This is a hand derivation from _assemble; it has not been checked numerically, though it is a one-line assertion to add to verify_forward.py.

2. The Geselowitz sensitivity relation

Let ud solve (1) and (2) for current Id driven on electrodes (a,b), and us solve the reciprocal problem, a hypothetical current Is driven on the sense pair (p,q). Perturb the admittivity by Δκ. Subtracting the weak forms of the perturbed and unperturbed problems and using the symmetry established above gives, to first order in Δκ,

(4)   ΔZ = − ∫Ω Δκ(r)   [∇ud(r)/Id] · [∇us(r)/Is]   dV  ≡  − ∫Ω Δκ(r) S(r) dV

where S(r) is the sensitivity distribution. Sign convention matters and is easy to get wrong, so it is stated explicitly: define the lead field Ed = −∇ud/Id (an electric field per unit current, units Ω·m−1) and likewise Es. Then S = Ed·Es = (∇ud/Id)·(∇us/Is), units Ω2·m−2, and S is invariant under flipping the sign convention on both fields. The symmetry of (4) under d↔s is Maxwell reciprocity; V2 is the discrete measurement of it.

Dimensional correction to the form quoted in the project brief. The relation is often written ΔZ = −∫ Δσ (Jd·Js)/(IdIs) dV. As written that is dimensionally inconsistent. J/I has units m−2, so Δσ [S·m−1] × m−4 = S·m−5, and after ×dV [m3] the integral carries S·m−2, not Ω. (An earlier draft of this box said the integral carried S; that was itself wrong, and is corrected here.) The consistent current-density form carries the background admittivity explicitly,

(5)   ΔZ = − ∫ (Δκ/κ2)   [Jd/Id] · [Js/Is]   dV,   (m/S)·m−4·m3 = Ω ✓

which reduces to (4) since J = κ∇u in a locally isotropic medium. (5) and (4) are the same equation under a change of variable, so the 1/κ2 is bookkeeping, not a mechanism, and an earlier draft was wrong to say "the 1/κ2 is why the fat shell is disproportionately sensitive". The correct argument is interface continuity: the normal component of J is continuous across the fat/muscle boundary while E = J/κ is not, so where current crosses the shell rather than running along it, E in the fat is about σmuscle/σfat = 0.34/0.043 = 7.9× the muscle value and S, being bilinear in two such fields, is elevated by up to 7.92 ≈ 62×. That factor is an analytic expectation only. Every number in §5 to §8 comes from a homogeneous half-space at σ = 0.34 S/m, which contains no fat shell and therefore cannot exhibit this effect at all. A two-layer half-space Green's function is the cheap way to quantify it and has not been computed.

Three properties of (4) are used below, each exact within the linearisation:

  1. Bilinearity in the two lead fields. Anything that perturbs either field perturbs the measurement, whether or not it perturbs the tissue. This is the whole of §4.
  2. The integrand is signed. S(r) < 0 wherever the drive and sense lead fields are antiparallel. This is §5, and its projected version in §6 is the more important one.
  3. Locality is set by geometry, not by the amplifier. S falls off as a high power of depth; how high depends on electrode separation. This is §7.

2.1 What the real-part projection actually retains

zone_series reads dZ.real.mean(axis=1), the real part of a complex ΔZ averaged over the two frequencies, and simulate_trial forms dZ = (Z − base)/np.abs(base). The division is by the magnitude of the reference, not by the complex reference, so the phase of Zref is not removed. In a homogeneous proxy where Z ∝ 1/κ, (4) gives ΔZ ≈ −Z(Δκ/κ)g with g real and positive, so arg ΔZ = arg Δκ − 2 arg κ and the retained fraction of the fractional excursion is

The danger is a differential rotation, one zone sitting over tissue with a materially different arg κ from its neighbours, because that flips relative sign between zones, and §6 shows relative sign is exactly what this estimator cannot survive.

The homogeneous Z ∝ 1/κ step behind (6) is a proxy. The actual model has muscle, a fat shell, kidney, bladder wall and a urine lumen in the sensed volume, and its zone reference impedance is not simply 1/κ of any one tissue. This project has not computed arg Zref per zone from the FEM, which is a one-line diagnostic on data the solver already returns. Until it is, η is an order-of-magnitude statement, and the possibility that a kidney-adjacent zone reports an inverted real part at 100 kHz is a live hypothesis rather than a finding.

4. Why tetrapolar sensing suppresses contact impedance, and why the rejection is only 4.3×

The intuitive argument is that the sense electrodes carry no current, so no I·z drop develops across their contact layer. In this model that argument is exactly true, and the consequence is stronger than usually stated. For a sense electrode l the electrode row of (3) with Il = 0 and γ = 0 (§1) reads

(8)   −(cl·u)/zl + (|El|/zl) Ul = 0   ⇒   Ul = (cl·u)/|El|

The 1/zl cancels identically. The sensed potential is the area-weighted mean of the boundary potential under the electrode footprint, with no explicit dependence on the sense electrode's contact impedance at all. The simulated voltmeter has infinite input impedance and perfect common-mode rejection ratio by construction.

And yet V4 measured a residual tetrapolar spread of 0.1621 over z0 ∈ [5, 20] Ω·cm2 against a bipolar spread of 0.7031, a rejection factor of only 4.34×. Since the explicit dependence is provably zero, every part of that 16% residual is implicit: it enters through u itself. Equation (4) names the mechanism precisely. Contact impedance does not appear in the sensitivity integrand, but it appears in both lead fields:

  • Drive-side current redistribution. The Robin term Ml/zl controls how strongly the drive electrode behaves as an equipotential. At small z0 the electrode is nearly a perfect conductor and the current density crowds towards its rim (the classic edge singularity); at large z0 the current density becomes nearly uniform over the footprint. Those are physically different sources, so Ed differs, S(r) differs, and ΔZ differs, even with the sense electrodes ideal.
  • Sense-side shunting. Zero net current does not mean zero local current. A sense electrode with finite zl is a partially conducting patch welded to the skin; it sinks current on one part of its footprint and sources it on another, netting zero. That circulating current perturbs u near the electrode. As zl → ∞ the electrode becomes a non-perturbing point probe; as zl → 0 it becomes a short-circuit patch that flattens the surface potential over its whole footprint. This is why draw_contact_z's docstring insists on the physical regime: values about 100× smaller make the electrodes near-ideal shunts that suppress the bolus signal while maximising drift sensitivity.
  • Finite footprint. Equation (8) is an average of u over the electrode, not a point value. The averaging kernel is fixed, but the field being averaged is the one the drive electrodes produced, so the drive-side term propagates through.

In real hardware there is a fourth mechanism this model does not contain at all: the instrumentation amplifier has finite differential and common-mode input impedance, so a genuine current i = (Up − Uq)/Zin flows through the sense contacts and develops a real i·zl error, and any mismatch zp ≠ zq converts common-mode drive potential into differential error at a rate set by the amplifier's CMRR and the source-impedance imbalance.

Correction to published language. build_methods.py states that contact impedance "largely drops out of the sensed quantity" and the strip3d.py module docstring says "contact impedance largely rejected". Both are overclaims and should be struck. The supported statement is: tetrapolar sensing reduces sensitivity to contact impedance by roughly a factor of four over the modelled range; a 16% residual sensitivity remains, and because the model's voltmeter is ideal, the true figure for real hardware can only be worse. The V4 residual is a lower bound on physical contact-impedance sensitivity, not an estimate of it.

Two limitations of V4 itself, and one withdrawal:

  • V4 sweeps a common contact impedance. zc = np.full(world.L, float(z0)) sets every electrode to the same value. That is the most benign case: a uniform z0 shift is close to common mode and is exactly what the across-zone mean subtraction in _strip_aperture_stats is designed to null. The trial generator does not do this. draw_contact_z draws z0 = rng.uniform(5, 20, size=world.L) independently per electrode, then applies per-electrode phase-randomised breathing modulation and a motion-scaled independent term. The differential-mismatch case, which is the one that actually degrades a tetrapolar measurement, was never isolated by V4. The obvious test is to hold the mean z0 fixed and sweep the spread, and it has not been run.
  • The 4.3× figure is mesh-conditioned. V4 ran on the n_rings=6, nz=17 mesh, which is V3's 2261-node level, where absolute |Z| carries a 12.4% error against the finest mesh. The contact term scales as z0/|El| with |El| the realised facet area, and V3's whole finding is that realised electrode area snaps to mesh facets and changes discontinuously with refinement (11 to 76% non-monotone swings in |Z|). The qualitative conclusion, that rejection is finite and modest rather than near-total, is robust. The number 4.34 should be read as "order 4", not as a calibrated constant.
  • Withdrawn. An earlier draft criticised V4 for using world.zones[:8] and thereby excluding "the one aperture doing the depth work". That criticism was wrong, and in fact backwards. tetrapolar_zones orders zones strip-major then G-major, so zones[:8] is exactly strip 0's five G=3 zones plus its three G=5 zones, that is, precisely the set of zones the estimator can use on that strip. The only zone excluded is the G=7 zone the estimator discards anyway. V4's subset is well chosen; the correct statement is that it covers one strip rather than both, and the two strips do not have mirror-image realised electrode area (a defect the fractional normalisation was introduced to handle), so repeating V4 on zones[9:17] would be worth the two minutes it costs.

5. Negative lobes in the raw zone sensitivity

Because S(r) = Ed·Es is an inner product of two vector fields, it changes sign wherever they are antiparallel. To locate the sign structure for the array the code actually builds, I evaluated (4) analytically for a homogeneous half-space using surface monopole Green's functions at the exact electrode positions place_strips and tetrapolar_zones produce. With u(r) = (ρ/2π) Σk sk/|r−rk| per unit current and ∇(1/|r−rk|) = −(r−rk)/|r−rk|3, the lead field defined in §2 is

(9)   E(r) = −∇u/I = +(ρ/2π) Σk sk (r−rk)/|r−rk|3,   ∇u/I = −(ρ/2π) Σk sk (r−rk)/|r−rk|3

An earlier draft printed the second form with the first form's sign. S is unaffected because both fields flip together, but the expression as printed was wrong and is corrected here.

Also note a geometry detail that the code's own docstrings get wrong: tetrapolar_zones describes "sliding Wenner zones", but the sense pair is (k+c, k+c+1), always adjacent, one pitch apart, whatever the outer gap G. So only G=3 is a Wenner-α array; G=5 is a Schlumberger array with AB/MN = 5. The array is collinear and longitudinal, not concentric: place_strips puts all electrodes at one azimuth and varies z. Those are exactly the tissues that translate under respiration and electrode motion, and the tissues where the interface argument of §2 predicts elevated S. A superficial perturbation straddling a sign boundary produces a bipolar, sign-ambiguous excursion whose fitted arrival time is meaningless. This remains a plausible contributor to the 11.1% false-retrograde floor on empty windows at zero motion measured in Study 6, but §6 gives a mechanism that needs no shallow tissue, no motion and no sensitivity-field argument at all, and that mechanism is now the leading candidate.

6. The operator the estimator actually applies: common-mode-rejected sensitivity

§5 analyses the sensitivity of a raw zone. The estimator never sees a raw zone. _strip_aperture_stats does, for every aperture with three or more zones,

if nz >= 3: ser = ser - ser.mean(axis=0, keepdims=True)

the across-zone mean of that strip at that aperture. The effective sensitivity of zone i is therefore not Si but

(10)   S̃i(r) = Si(r) − (1/n) Σj Sj(r),   Σi S̃i(r) ≡ 0 for all r

The identity on the right is the whole problem. The projected sensitivity sums to zero across zones at every point in space and every frame, so any perturbation anywhere, at any depth, produces a positive-going residual in some zones and a negative replica in others. This is a first-order, at-depth, guaranteed sign inversion between zones. It requires no negative lobe, no shallow tissue and no motion. The code's own comment already concedes the degenerate limit: "with exactly 2 the mean-removed series are exact negatives and the lag is destroyed". At nz = 3, which is G=5, the deepest usable aperture, the projection removes one of only three degrees of freedom.

6.1 The estimator is maximally exposed to relative sign, not protected from it

An earlier draft stated that "the estimator is protected because it uses only timing, never sign or amplitude of the excursion". That is false twice over and is withdrawn.

  • Timing is computed from signed traces. xcorr_lag calls _norm(a) = (a − mean)/std, which is mean-and-scale normalisation, not rectification, and then maximises c[L] = dot(a, b)/n over L ∈ [−max_lag, +max_lag] with max_lag = max(3, 2n//3), which is ±13 samples for the T = 20 both cited studies use. If zone j's projected excursion is inverted relative to zone 0, no lag makes the correlation positive, so argmax lands wherever the biased /n taper drives c least negative, which is at the extreme of the search range. A relative sign flip does not degrade the lag by a little, it replaces it with a saturated ±13 value that then dominates the least-squares slope. Sign is the single most load-bearing property of this estimator.
  • Amplitude is used explicitly. direction_features weights each aperture by z_baseline × (0.25 + lin) × √dif_energy, and decide_direction selects the strip by dif_energy. Amplitude selects the strip and weights the fusion.

The corrected statement of the protection, then, is:

  • The timing formulation gives no protection against relative sign inversion between zones. It converts it into saturated lags.
  • The only protection is downstream: the lin gate at 0.35, which is an abstain rule, plus the (0.25 + lin) aperture weighting inside the fusion.
  • Because the CMR projection (10) guarantees sign inversion between inner and outer zones of every aperture, that gate is not a nicety, it is structural. Removing it would not slightly degrade performance, it would expose the estimator directly to a first-order sign flip.

This is now the leading candidate explanation for the 11.1% zero-motion false-retrograde floor that Study 6 measured on empty windows, superseding the shallow-lobe hypothesis of §5(3). It needs no motion, no shallow tissue and no sensitivity-field geometry: mean subtraction across three zones guarantees anti-correlated pairs, xcorr_lag saturates on anti-correlated pairs at ±13, and a saturated lag pair fitted against zone height yields a large-magnitude slope whose sign is essentially arbitrary. The project has not tested this. The test is cheap and decisive: take the empty (no-flow) windows already generated by run_motion2.py and record what fraction of tarr entries sit at exactly ±13, and what the false-retrograde rate is conditional on that. If the floor is saturation-driven, clipping max_lag to the physically possible transit range, or discarding saturated lags rather than regressing them, should move the floor directly. Neither has been tried.

The proxy above is a half-space, noiseless, with a point bolus and no fat shell, cylinder, end caps or contact impedance. Its per-aperture sign statistics should not be read as predictions of the FEM results; they establish that the mechanism exists and is first-order, not its magnitude in the real model.

7. Depth sensitivity versus electrode separation

Two figures of merit must be distinguished, and conflating them is the standard error in this area.

Absolute sensitivity S = Ed·Es determines ΔZ in ohms and is maximised by short arrays, which concentrate both lead fields. Fractional sensitivity S/Z determines ΔZ/|Z|, which is what the code actually carries, because simulate_trial normalises dZ = (Z − base)/np.abs(base) and, decisively, injects fractional noise σn = 10−SNR/20 on the normalised quantity.

An earlier draft justified the wide aperture by "Z falls roughly as 1/G while S falls more slowly". That exponent is wrong, and as written it refutes its own conclusion. For a symmetric collinear array on a half-space with drives at ±Ga/2 and sense at ±a/2, superposing the four monopole terms gives exactly

(11)   Z = (ρ/2π)[1/AM − 1/BM − 1/AN + 1/BN] = 4ρ / (π a (G2 − 1))

So Z falls as 1/(G2−1), not 1/G. At ρ = 294.1 Ω·cm and a = 2.286 cm that is Z = 20.48, 6.83, 3.41 Ω for G = 3, 5, 7. Over the usable range G = 3 → 5 the denominator goes 8 → 24, so Z falls exactly 3.00×, while absolute S at 3.025 cm falls only 1.70× (10.63 → 6.26 Ω2·cm−2). The ratio, 1.77×, is exactly the S/Z increase the table below shows (0.5188 → 0.9174). Had Z fallen only as 1/G (1.67×) the narrow aperture would have won. The wide-aperture conclusion survives only with the correct exponent.

V3 supplies the independent justification for working in fractional terms at all: absolute |Z| does not converge (11 to 76% non-monotone error over an 8× node range, because realised electrode area snaps to mesh facets) while the arrival-time slope converges cleanly (6.8 → 2.8 → 0.97 → 0.11%). No claim about absolute impedance magnitude is supported by this model; only differential and timing claims are. Note that this cuts against (11) too: (11) is an analytic half-space result, not a model output, and the model's own |Z| is not converged enough to check it against.

The real domain is a 5.5 cm-radius cylinder with insulating end caps, so current is confined and forced around the body, and contralateral current density is strictly higher than the proxy predicts. The error is largest exactly where it matters: the contralateral distance of 7.975 cm exceeds the domain radius, so the proxy is evaluated well outside any regime where a half-space is even approximately the right geometry. Side assignment is what makes this device clinically actionable (VUR is unilateral in roughly half of cases and management differs), so this is not a corner case. Two CEM solves, one with a bolus on each side, would settle it directly and cheaply. They have not been run.

8.1 Corrections that apply to every number in those tables

  1. These are raw-zone excursions. The estimator sees the CMR projection. Applying the retention factors from §6 (0.34 at 16 cm G=5, 0.28 at 10 cm G=5) and the matching noise reduction √(1−1/3) = 0.82, the grade-I per-sample margins at 3.025 cm fall from 10.0× to about 4.2× (16 cm) and from 7.25× to about 2.5× (10 cm).
  2. Real-part projection and frequency averaging. §2.1 gives a net factor of 1.10× in SNR (signal ×0.777, independent noise ×0.707). Applying it: about 4.6× and 2.7×.
  3. Point-perturbation bias, and its direction. Every excursion above evaluates S at the bolus centre and treats it as constant over the bolus volume. That is a real approximation, but it biases the numbers low, not high: because S falls as a high power of depth it is convex, so the shallow half of the bolus gains more than the deep half loses. Monte-Carlo integration over the actual ellipsoid gives ⟨S⟩/S(centre) = 1.02 (grade I, 16 cm G=5) to 1.31 (grade III, 10 cm G=3). So the centre-point estimate is conservative by 2 to 31%, and an objection that it sits at the sensitivity maximum has the sign backwards.
  4. The 5 to 10× "coherent gain available to a timing estimate" claimed in an earlier draft is deleted. It was invented. The two frequencies are already consumed inside zone_series; the T = 20 frames are the signal support, not repeats; the zones of a strip span apertures that share electrodes and sample overlapping volumes, so they are strongly correlated rather than independent; and the CMR subtraction removes a rank-one component of the very signal being integrated.

The amplitude margin does not establish that the direction call is noise-robust, and this section does not compute the quantity that would. Delay-estimator variance scales as 1/(SNR2·βrms2) with βrms the RMS bandwidth of the zone waveform. The waveform here is a slow unipolar passage across T = 20 frames, which is the lowest-bandwidth and therefore worst case for delay estimation, and the output of interest is the sign of a regression slope through those delays, not the delays themselves. The Cramér-Rao bound on arrival-time variance, and its propagation into P(sign error) for the least-squares slope, is the missing calculation. It is analytic and cheap.

Study 6 already gives direct evidence that amplitude SNR is not what binds: an 11.1% false-retrograde rate on empty windows at zero motion and 60 dB SNR, precisely the regime these tables call comfortable. Either that floor is not noise, in which case these margins are irrelevant to performance, or it is, in which case these margins are wrong. Either way, §8 licenses no performance claim. The honest headline is: the half-space proxy puts the grade-I excursion at the modelled depth roughly 10× (16 cm) or 7× (10 cm) above the assumed per-sample instrument noise floor before common-mode rejection, and roughly 4.6× and 2.7× after it. Whether that survives the fat shell, curvature, insulating end caps and finite contact impedance is not known, and the two CEM solves per zone that would settle it have not been run.

9. Reconciling the lead-field picture with the Study 5 placement result

The lead-field analysis says the 16 cm strip is the deeper instrument: 38% more fractional sensitivity at the ureter at the deepest usable aperture, and a 2.08 cm rather than 1.30 cm lobe-closure depth. Study 5 says the opposite about clinical performance. The 16 cm strip at z = 0.50 never detects 25 to 33% of refluxing children on any of six voids, and 43% of low-grade refluxers even at perfect placement, while the 10 cm strip over the ureterovesical junction at z = 0.34 misses 0% at perfect placement and 8% at 2 cm of placement error. These are not in conflict, and the resolution is the central design lesson:

  • Depth reach is not the binding constraint. Axial coverage is. A zone with excellent depth sensitivity centred 6 cm above the ureterovesical junction sees nothing, because low-grade reflux never travels far enough to enter its sensitivity volume. GRADE_TABLE[1][1] = 0.20 and [2][1] = 0.38 cap the retrograde reach at 20% and 38% of the bladder-to-kidney distance. Concretely, with z_bladder = 4 cm, z_kidney = 16 cm and a 2.4 cm margin, a grade I bolus traverses z = 1.6 to 8.8 cm. The 10 cm strip at z = 0.34 spans 1.8 to 11.8 cm, so the bolus crosses most of it; the 16 cm strip at z = 0.50 spans 2 to 18 cm, so the bolus never reaches the upper half of the array and those zones contribute noise and a zero-mean CMR pedestal rather than signal. The 16 cm strip's superiority in S/Z is spent on tissue with no bolus in it.
  • The estimator's own weighting fights the deep aperture, and equation (7) says why. On a fixed span the widest usable aperture has the shortest lever arm: 4.57 cm against 9.14 cm on the 16 cm strip, 2.86 cm against 5.71 cm on the 10 cm strip. Since direction_features weights by z_baseline × (0.25 + lin) × √dif_energy, the aperture with the best depth figure of merit is systematically down-weighted. Fusion rather than selection is the only defensible resolution, and the code does fuse.
  • The design implication is a longer strip that is still centred low, i.e. more electrodes over the ureterovesical junction, which buys both axial coverage and, through the multi-aperture synthesis in tetrapolar_zones, a deep aperture in software with enough zones to survive the nz < 2 filter. That is a hypothesis the sweep has not run. Study 5 varied span and centre together (10 cm at z = 0.34 versus 16 cm at z = 0.50) and cannot separate them. The missing cells are 16 cm at z = 0.34 and 10 cm at z = 0.50, and they are the cheapest informative runs left in the project.

The Study 5 cells are n = 48 children each and the 16 cm arm is non-monotone in placement error (sensitivity 75, 67, 88, 83% for σ = 0, 0.5, 1, 2 cm), which is not physically sensible and shows the per-cell noise is large. The never-detected gap (0 to 8% versus 25 to 33%) is large and consistent across cells, but the sensitivity and specificity numbers individually should not be quoted to two significant figures. Note also that the §6 proxy reproduces the qualitative Study 5 ordering: on the 10 cm strip at z = 0.34 the proxy calls grades I, II and III correctly, while on the 16 cm strip at z = 0.50 it abstains on grade II. That agreement is suggestive, not confirmatory: it is a noiseless half-space with no anatomy, and it disagrees with Study 5 at grade V on the 10 cm strip.

10. What is assumed rather than tested, in this section specifically

  1. The sensitivity field was never computed with the actual FEM operator. Every S(r), S/Z, S̃, negative-lobe boundary, depth exponent, crossover, cross-talk ratio and grade excursion above comes from an analytic homogeneous half-space Green's-function evaluation at the code's realised electrode positions, not from CEM3D. It ignores the finite cylinder and its insulating end caps 2 cm beyond the 16 cm array; surface curvature; the 0.99 cm fat shell at 1/7.9 the muscle conductivity, whose interface amplification §2 estimates at up to 62× in S; the kidney and bladder inclusions; and the CEM's finite contact impedance. The direction and rough magnitude of each of these is arguable; their net effect on the numbers is not known. Computing S directly is cheap, two solves per zone and an element-wise product, and would replace this entire section's arithmetic with measurement. It should be done before these numbers are relied upon. The arithmetic itself has been independently recomputed for this revision and reproduces the earlier tables to three figures where the geometry is the same, so the errors corrected here are errors of which array and which operator, not of calculation.
  2. γ ≡ 0 is a hand derivation from the structure of _assemble, not a numerical check. It is the load-bearing step in the claim that the model's voltmeter is ideal, and hence in the claim that the V4 16% residual is a lower bound on physical contact-impedance sensitivity.
  3. V2 is a symmetry and solver check, not a physics validation (§1). There is no analytic or phantom benchmark anywhere in this project.
  4. V4 tested common-mode contact impedance only, on one strip, on a mesh where |Z| carries 12.4% discretisation error (§4). The differential-mismatch case, which is the one that degrades tetrapolar sensing and the one the trial generator actually produces, is untested.
  5. The CMR sign-inversion mechanism of §6 is derived and demonstrated in the proxy, but never measured in the model. Equation (10) is exact and its consequence, Σi S̃i = 0, is unavoidable. What is not established is the resulting rate of saturated lags in the real FEM trials. The diagnostic is a two-line addition to _strip_aperture_stats.
  6. Tissue values are literature, not measured, and the dispersion model in admittivity(), σ(f) = σ0(1 + 0.04 log10(f/50 kHz)) and εr(f) = εr0(50 kHz/f)0.10, is a two-parameter fit over a single octave, not a Cole-Cole or Gabriel-parameterised model. Over 50 to 100 kHz that is defensible; it should not be extrapolated. §2.1's inversion result depends on ωε/σ for kidney, which is the least well constrained of these.
  7. σurine is fixed at 1.70 S/m in every trial ever run (§8.3), and every excursion is exactly linear in it.
  8. The 60 dB SNR is an assumed budget, not a datasheet number, and §8.2 argues it is probably not the binding floor in any case.
  9. No patient or phantom data of any kind exists for this device. The linearisation in (4) is first-order in Δκ, and for grade IV to V boluses (ΔZ/|Z| computed above at 10 to 27%) it is being pushed past where a Born-type approximation is quantitatively trustworthy. The FEM solve is fully nonlinear and does not use (4); (4) is used here only as an interpretive tool, which is the one role it can safely play.
  10. No delay-estimation error analysis exists (§8.1). Amplitude margin has been computed; arrival-time variance and P(slope sign error) have not.

Finally, a note of calibration for the reader. This project has now had five confident claims overturned by measurement, including the authors' own nominated existential risk, the prediction that a craniocaudal respiratory gradient would inject a phase-locked false travelling wave, refuted by Study 6 where the full-gradient arm was if anything slightly better (false-retrograde 14.4% and direction accuracy 79.4% at grad = 1.0, against 15.8% and 71.9% at grad = 0.0). This revision adds several more, all of them errors in the previous draft of this section: a depth argument built on an aperture the estimator discards, a claim of sign-independence contradicted by four lines of the estimator, a lead-field analysis performed on the wrong operator, a 1/G scaling that refuted its own conclusion, a real-part retention figure computed against the wrong normalisation, a dimensional correction that was itself dimensionally wrong, a criticism of V4's zone subset that was exactly backwards, and a cross-talk conclusion drawn from an upper bound. The correct prior on the remaining analytic arguments here is therefore not high. They are offered as a framework for interpreting the simulation, and the specific numbers should be regarded as provisional until the sensitivity field is computed with the real operator.

3.4  Why direction is observable, and why one symmetric array is not enough

The device does not reconstruct a conductivity field. It answers a one-bit question about transport: did the conductive bolus travel toward the kidney or toward the bladder. This section states the estimator that answers it, the conditions under which that answer is identifiable, the symmetry argument that forces a longitudinal strip rather than a ring, and three places where the implementation in directional_sim.py falls short of the claim. One of those places is a live threat to a published placement conclusion.

1. The travelling-wave model

Take from the lead-field section the first-order sensitivity relation for a tetrapolar zone j, which we use only in the compact form

   ΔZj(t) = − ∫Ω Δσ(r,t) Kj(r) dV    (1)

with Kj the lead-field product of zone j. In bolus_path the perturbation is a rigid slug of urine of fixed radii (br, br, 1.15*br) whose centre moves along the ureter at constant axial speed: the axial coordinates are zs = z0 + (z1 - z0) * np.linspace(0,1,T), so ẑ = v is exactly constant and the shape is exactly time-invariant. Then Δσ(r,t) = ψ(r − rb(t)) and (1) becomes a function of bolus position alone:

   ΔZj(t) = − gj(z(t)),   gj = (Kj ∗ ψ) evaluated on the ureter line    (2)

Zones of one aperture on a straight strip differ, to the extent the kernel is translation-covariant along z, only by an axial offset and a gain: Kj(x,y,z) ≈ aj K(x,y,z − ζj), where ζj is the zone centroid — in code world.zone_z, the mean realised facet height of the sense pair. Substituting,

   ΔZj(t) = − aj h( v (t − τj) ),   τj = (ζj − z0) / v    (3)

Every zone sees the same waveform h, scaled by an unknown gain aj and delayed by τj. Differentiating the delay against the regressor the code actually uses,

   dτ / dζ = 1 / v,   so  sign(dτ/dζ) = sign(v)    (4)

This is the whole diagnosis. _strip_aperture_stats estimates τj by cross-correlating each zone against the lowest zone (xcorr_lag(ser[0], ser[j])), regresses tarr on zz = world.zone_z[idx] by least squares, and decide_direction returns the sign of ev = f["lag"] + f["slope"]. Positive slope means later arrival higher up, means travelling superior, means retrograde.

Equation (3) also explains the mesh-convergence result. The unknown gains aj enter the amplitude and cancel out of τj. Electrode patches snap to mesh facets, so realised electrode area — and with it |Z| — jumps discontinuously under refinement (11–76% error, oscillating, 704→5675 nodes), while the arrival-time slope converged monotonically at 6.80, 2.84, 0.97, 0.11% and the decision was invariant on all five meshes. That is not luck; it is the structure of (3). It also means no absolute impedance claim in this work is supported, only differential and timing ones.

2. Identifiability conditions, stated explicitly

  1. Non-separability. If the response factorised as aj × e(t) — one shared envelope, per-zone amplitude — then all τj are equal and (4) is vacuous. bolus_path avoids this by construction: frac[:] = 1.0, no rise/fall envelope, the slug enters and leaves the sensed span. Real reflux, which fills and empties, is not guaranteed to be non-separable. Untested.
  2. Monotone trajectory. z(t) must be monotone over the window or τj is multivalued. linspace enforces it. Physiological reflux is oscillatory across a fill–void cycle. Untested.
  3. Distinct, ordered, sufficiently spread ζj. Slope precision scales with the axial lever arm, not with electrode separation, which is why direction_features weights each aperture by z_baseline.
  4. Gain-blindness. The estimator must use only timing. It does; this is what makes it survive the non-convergence of |Z| and the 4.3× (not infinite) tetrapolar rejection of contact impedance over z0 ~ U(5,20) Ω·cm².
  5. Removable common mode. Requires ≥3 zones (section 4).
  6. Reference-independence. Zref is the mean of the first two frames, a per-zone complex constant. Adding an arbitrary such constant to dZ moves the decision evidence by 8.9e−16: every path ends in a mean removal, so the constant cancels exactly. The sign is therefore reference-free. The gain 1/|Zref| and the energy feature are not.
  7. Adequate temporal sampling. The required lag must fit inside the estimator's search window with enough overlap to be estimable. This condition is violated in one published arm — section 7.

3. A single symmetric tetrapolar zone is direction-blind

In tetrapolar_zones the outer gap G is odd and the sense pair sits at offset c = (G-1)//2, i.e. electrodes (k+c, k+c+1) straddling the zone midpoint. The four-electrode configuration is therefore mirror-symmetric about ζj. Let R be the reflection z ↦ 2ζj − z. R maps the drive pair to itself and the sense pair to itself (swapping its two members, which only flips the polarity of V, a fixed sign convention). In a medium that is itself mirror-symmetric about ζj, Kj(Rr) = Kj(r), so from (2)

   ΔZj(−v)(t) = ΔZj(+v)(t0 − t)    (5)

The single-zone trace of an ascending bolus is the time-reverse of a descending one. Every statistic invariant under time reversal — peak, energy, dwell, area, spectral magnitude — is identical for the two directions. One symmetric zone cannot carry the bit, no matter how good the front end is.

Reciprocity sharpens the point rather than duplicating it: the verification found max relative reciprocity error 4.77e−15 over 12 zones, so swapping drive and sense reproduces the same Z. No static tetrapolar reading has an intrinsic orientation. Direction can only live in the time ordering across spatially separated zones, which is exactly what (4) extracts.

This is the geometric argument for a strip over a belt. A circumferential ring of electrodes at one height replicates zones around φ at a common ζ. All τj are then equal, the regressor zz is constant, the lever arm is zero and dτ/dζ is undefined. Channel count does not help: 32 electrodes on one ring buy angular resolution, which is what tomography needs, and zero axial lever arm, which is what direction needs. Laterality then comes for free from the second strip: place_strips puts them at strip_phis=(0.0, np.pi), i.e. on ±x, and the ureters sit at x = ±0.45R, so each strip is nearest its ipsilateral ureter. Note the parity asymmetry of the two outputs: which side is chosen by dif_energy, an even (presence) statistic, while which way is the sign of an odd (timing) statistic. Laterality is only trustworthy because dZ was made fractional — with absolute dZ the strip selector saw ~7× more "energy" on the smaller-area strip with no bolus present, a degenerate always-one-side predictor.

4. Three zones is the minimum, not two

Two zones give a lag and therefore a sign. They fail on three separate counts, all visible in _strip_aperture_stats:

  • Common mode cannot be rejected. Model ser[j,t] = c(t) + b(t − τj). The shared term c (contact drift, breathing, correlated instrument noise, bulk motion) plants a large lag-0 cross-correlation peak that pulls every τ̂ toward zero. Removing the across-zone mean kills it — but with exactly two zones, s1 − mean = (s1−s2)/2 and s2 − mean = −(s1−s2)/2 are exact negatives, whose cross-correlation argmax is the minimum of ρ and is noise-placed. So a 2-zone aperture must either keep c and be biased to lag 0, or remove c and lose the lag entirely. The code takes the first branch (if nz >= 3: ser = ser - ser.mean(axis=0)).
  • No goodness-of-fit, so no abstain gate. Two points fit a line exactly; the code sets lin = 0.0 for nz<3 and decide_direction only applies LIN_GATE when n_zones >= 3. A 2-zone aperture is therefore ungated, and an empty window is forced to a call. Measured on no-flow windows, the ungated detector said "retrograde" 53.8% of the time.
  • No residual, so no way to tell a wave from a wobble. Linearity of τ against ζ is the only evidence that a coherent travelling perturbation existed at all.

Since a strip of N electrodes yields N−G zones at gap G, three zones at the narrowest odd gap needs N ≥ 6. The locked design uses N=8, giving 5 zones at G=3, 3 at G=5, and 1 at G=7.

Discrepancy: the deepest aperture is silently discarded. At N=8, G=7 yields one zone per strip, and _strip_aperture_stats returns None for nz < 2. So the deepest synthesised aperture — the design's stated reason for adding electrodes — contributes nothing. Meanwhile StripWorld publishes max_aperture_cm = max(_g) * spacing = 7 × pitch, i.e. 10.0 cm on the 10 cm strip, when the deepest aperture that survives is G=5 = 7.14 cm. The reported sensing depth overstates the realised one by 40%.

Discrepancy: "two independent estimators" is not true. direction_features documents lag and slope as "two independent estimators of the same sign", and decide_direction sums them. But lag = (tarr[-1]-tarr[0])/dz is the endpoint slope through the same tarr vector the least-squares slope is fitted to. For 5 equally spaced zones the two are weighted sums with weights (−¼,0,0,0,¼) and (−.2,−.1,0,.1,.2); under i.i.d. timing errors their correlation is 0.894. ev is therefore close to 1.9× a single estimator, not a two-vote fusion. Harmless for a sign test, fatal for any variance or confidence statement built on the independence claim.

5. "You are detecting presence, not direction"

This is the strongest objection and it deserves a formal answer, not a rebuttal. From (5), the separation is exactly a parity under time reversal. Presence statistics — energy, dif_energy, peak, dwell — are even: reversing the frame order leaves them unchanged. Direction statistics are odd: tarr ↦ −tarr, so lag, slope and ev all flip. decide_direction is built only from odd statistics (lin, the gate, is even, but it gates rather than signs). To that extent the objection is answered by construction.

It is not fully answered, for two reasons that the code makes plain:

  • feature_vector emits log_energy, log_dif_energy, energy_asym and abs_lag_max alongside the odd features. Any classifier trained on the full vector can and will read presence. The direction-only claim holds for decide_direction; it does not automatically transfer to classifier metrics computed on the full vector.
  • The generative model leaks speed. From bolus_path, a reflux bolus of grade g travels D = 12r + 4.8 cm over T−1 = 19 frames, whereas every antegrade bolus travels the full 16.8 cm. So |dτ/dζ| = 19/D is 2.64 frames/cm for grade I and fixed at 1.13 for all antegrade trials. Slope magnitude is label-informative. Sign-only decision is immune; a learned classifier is not.

The experiment that would settle it is cheap and is not in verify_forward.py. Take each already-simulated antegrade trial, reverse the frame index of dZ, re-run decide_direction, and require a sign flip. No new forward solves — it is an array re-index. A true direction estimator flips on 100% of trials; a presence estimator's call is unchanged; anything in between quantifies the leak. The same test diagnoses the unexplained 11.1% false-retrograde floor at zero motion in Study 6: for a parity-clean estimator, false-retrograde on antegrade must equal false-antegrade on reflux under exact reversal. A floor that survives reversal is an estimator asymmetry, not physics. The physical version — a peristaltic phantom pumped both ways at matched flow — has not been built. There is no phantom or patient data of any kind.

6. Bilateral reflux is structurally unreportable

decide_direction opens with s = max((0,1), key=...dif_energy...) and returns a single (sign, strip, evidence) triple. There is no representation for "both sides refluxing". This is not a tuning gap; the return type cannot express it. Worse, it is also untested: simulate_trial takes a scalar side, so every trial in every study is unilateral by construction. VCUG grades each ureter separately, and bilateral disease is common, so a device whose output has no bilateral state does not meet the stated clinical requirement.

The repair is small — decide per strip with independent gates and take the subject-level call as an OR — but it is not free. Two independent gates roughly double per-event false-positive exposure. Using the Study 6 per-event false-retrograde rates and assuming independence between strips, an OR rule gives 1−(1−0.111)² = 21.0% at the zero-motion floor and 1−(1−0.158)² = 29.1% at grad 0. The independence assumption is itself untested: both strips see the same body, the same breathing and the same common mode, so the true figure may be lower. Every published specificity number would have to be re-derived after this change.

7. The max_lag clip, and a threat to the Study 5 placement conclusion

xcorr_lag defaults to max_lag = max(3, (2*n)//3). With T = 20 frames in both run_placement.py and run_precision.py, this is a hard clip at ±13 samples. The estimator is also deliberately biased (division by n, not by the overlap n−|L|), so its response tapers by roughly (1−|L|/n): at |L| = 13 of 20 only 35% of the record overlaps.

Combining (4) with the geometry, the end-to-end lag an aperture must resolve is L = B / v = B (T−1) / D, with B the aperture's axial baseline. The clip binds when

   B / D > (2T/3) / (T−1) = 13/19 = 0.684    (6)

At N=8 the G=3 aperture spans 4 electrode pitches: B = 5.71 cm on a 10 cm strip, B = 9.14 cm on a 16 cm strip. Required lags, in frames:

Saturation preserves sign, so it does not by itself flip a call; it compresses the arrival-time spread and flattens the fit. Worked example: if the three upper zones all saturate at 13, tarr = (0,13,13,13,13) on equally spaced zz gives a fitted R² of 0.50 — still above LIN_GATE = 0.35, so saturation alone does not force an abstain. This is a threat to the Study 5 conclusion, not a refutation of it. The 16 cm penalty may be partly an estimator artifact of a fixed 20-frame window rather than a fact about where the strip should sit. Three pieces of evidence point that way: the gap is already 25% never-detected at zero placement error, which placement precision cannot explain; the 16 cm arm is non-monotonic in σ (25/33/12/17%), which is what a roughly constant floor plus sampling noise looks like; and (6) predicts the failure to be concentrated in low grades, which is where it is observed. Against that: grade I saturates on the 10 cm strip too (15.1 > 13) yet 10 cm shows 0% low-grade-never, so saturation cannot be the whole story; the depth of saturation differs (1.16× over the clip at 10 cm versus 1.43–1.86× at 16 cm for grades I–II); and on both spans the narrow G=5 aperture stays unsaturated and keeps contributing. The numbers above also use nominal bladder/kidney heights and nominal pitch, while draw_anatomy jitters organ positions and realised pitch differs from nominal (2.50–3.14 cm measured against 2.80 nominal), so treat them as a threshold calculation, not an exact prediction.

The decisive test is to rerun the 16 cm arm with max_lag = n - 1 and, separately, with T raised so that B/D ≤ 1/3 (giving ≥67% record overlap at the required lag). If the never-detected gap survives both, the placement finding stands and the design should move to 10 cm @ z=0.34. If it collapses, the published locked design was never the problem and the estimator's time window was. Until that is run, the Study 5 conclusion should be reported as confounded with an estimator-window effect. A second reporting gap makes this harder than it needs to be: "never-detected" pools abstentions with wrong-direction calls, so the current metrics cannot separate a gate-driven failure from a sign-driven one. Abstain and mis-call should be logged separately.

8. What is assumed rather than tested

  • Translation covariance of Kj along z, assumed in (3). Only approximately true near the strip ends, where the cylinder terminates. Testable by evaluating the kernel directly on the ureter line for each zone and checking the shift-invariance residual.
  • Constant bolus speed and shape. Real ureteric transport is peristaltic and bolus volume changes; bolus_path's constant-v slug is the most favourable case (I2).
  • LIN_GATE = 0.35 is one hand-set constant, calibrated once by Youden at 0.45 cm motion and never swept anywhere in the repo. Section 7 shows the decision margin against it is span- and grade-dependent, which makes a single global threshold a poor fit.
  • Body habitus. draw_anatomy varies only organ positions. Torso radius, torso height, fat thickness, organ sizes and every conductivity including urine are identical across all "simulated children". No result here supports generalisation across habitus.
  • Grade prevalence. sample_grade() and GRADE_WEIGHTS are published as the prevalence model but sample_grade is never called; run_precision draws grades uniformly, giving 40% grade IV–V against the project's own declared 18%. Since required lag (table above) is a strong function of grade, this over-weights the easy end and flatters both arms of Study 5.
  • Statistical power. n = 48 per cell. A 0% cell has a 95% upper bound of about 6.3% by the rule of three, so "0% versus 25%" is a real gap but "0% versus 0%" between adjacent σ is not evidence of anything.

3.5  Common-mode rejection and the refuted gradient hypothesis

The estimator never looks at an impedance. It looks at the ordering in time of per-zone perturbations, and it gets there through two centering operations inside _strip_aperture_stats. This section does the algebra of those two operations, states exactly which disturbances they annihilate and which they cannot, derives the one disturbance class predicted to defeat them — a craniocaudal motion gradient — and then reports that Study 6 measured that prediction and refuted it. The refutation is the authors' own stated existential risk for the method, and it did not materialise.

1. What the across-zone mean actually removes

Let a strip-and-aperture group contain N zones with realised sense-midpoint heights z1 < … < zN (world.zone_z, taken from meshed facet centroids, not requested positions). Write the per-zone series ser[j,t], formed in zone_series as Re {dZ} averaged over the two excitation frequencies, as

rj(t) = c(t) + aj + s(t − τj) + nj(t)   (1)

with c(t) a per-frame term shared by every zone (contact drift, bulk body motion, correlated instrument noise), aj a per-zone constant (calibration offset, residual reference error), and s(t − τj) the travelling bolus term whose arrival times τj carry the entire diagnosis. The code applies

ser −= ser.mean(axis=0)  (across zones, per frame, only if nz ≥ 3)   (2)
ser −= ser.mean(axis=1)  (across time, per zone, always)   (3)

Operation (2) is the orthogonal projector Pz = IN − (1/N) 11T acting on the zone index; (3) is Pt acting on the time index. Applied to (1) in matrix form R = 1cT + a1T + S + N,

Pz R Pt = Pz S Pt + Pz N Pt   (4)

because Pz1 = 0 kills the common frame term and 1TPt = 0 kills the per-zone constant. Double centering annihilates exactly the additive rank-one family c(t) + aj and nothing more. This is the formal content of the audited no-op result: adding an arbitrary per-zone complex constant to dZ moves the decision evidence by 8.9×10−16, i.e. by the floating-point residue of (3). It is also why the resting-reference subtraction cannot change the direction call — base = Z[:2].mean(axis=0) contributes a per-zone constant, which (3) removes. The reference is not useless: the gain 1/|Zref| and the energy and dif_energy features, which drive strip selection and the aperture fusion weights, all survive it.

What (4) does not remove is equally important. The signal term becomes s(t − τj) − (1/N)Σk s(t − τk): the bolus is partly subtracted from itself, since the common-mode estimate is built from the very zones that contain it (§7). And any multiplicative disturbance f(zj) d(t) with non-constant f and non-constant d survives double centering intact. That observation is the hinge of §5.

2. Two zones cannot reject common mode — and the N=5 corollary

Set N = 2 in (2). The mean is (r1+r2)/2, so

r̃1 = (r1 − r2)/2,  r̃2 = (r2 − r1)/2 = −r̃1   (5)

The two mean-removed series are exact negatives. The rank of Pz is N−1 = 1, so the pair spans a one-dimensional space and carries one waveform, not two. Every lag xcorr_lag could have estimated is gone: with b = −a, after the internal _norm, c(L) = −ρaa(L), whose maximum over |L| ≤ max_lag = 2n/3 is attained wherever the autocorrelation is most negative. For a smooth unipolar bolus passage that is the edge of the search window, and its sign is set by whichever tail of the noise happens to be larger. The estimate is not merely zero — it is saturated and sign-arbitrary. Hence the guard if nz >= 3, whose in-code comment states the same conclusion: “a 2-zone aperture cannot reject common mode … a structural property of the geometry, not a tuning choice.”

Now push it through tetrapolar_zones. For Nelec electrodes per strip the odd outer gaps are range(3, N, 2) and aperture G yields N − G zones. At N = 5 the only gap is G = 3, giving exactly two zones and one aperture. Three consequences follow mechanically, and they compound:

  • Line (2) is skipped, so c(t) passes untouched into the lag estimator, where it contributes a lag-zero peak that competes directly with the travelling term.
  • lin is hard-set to 0.0 (“2 points are trivially collinear”), so no wave-linearity evidence exists.
  • decide_direction gates on f["n_zones"] >= 3 and f["lin"] < lin_gate. With n_zones = 2 the gate is never evaluated, so a 5-electrode strip can never abstain: it emits ±1 on every window, including empty ones, using a lag statistic with no common-mode rejection.

At N = 4 the single zone leaves nz < 2, defined = False, and the device abstains always — structurally undecidable, and honest about it. N = 5 is the dangerous case: confident, ungated, unprotected. The Study 6 configuration uses n_strip = 8, giving zone counts 5 / 3 / 1 at G = 3 / 5 / 7 — two usable apertures, the deepest discarded for having one zone.

3. The gradient hypothesis, stated properly

The motion model in base_sigma and add_bolus displaces tissue by a height-weighted vector, w = (1−g) + g z/H with H = 20 cm, so the displacement of material at height z is

u(z,t) = d(t) [(1−g) + g z/H]   (6)

Over a sensed span with zone centroid z̄, decompose (6) about that centroid:

u(zj,t) = d(t) w̄ + (g d(t)/H)(zj − z̄),   w̄ = (1−g) + g z̄/H   (7)

The first term is uniform across zones: a rigid translation, precisely the disturbance Pz annihilates, and precisely what draw_motion produced in every study before Study 6 (“COMMON across every zone and both strips”). A rigid-only motion model can therefore only ever confirm common-mode rejection; it is a tautology dressed as a result, and the code says so. The second term is zero-mean and linear in z, so Pz leaves it untouched.

The predicted damage runs through arrival time. A zone at lab height zj meets the bolus when the bolus, advected with the tissue at speed v, reaches material coordinate zj + u(zj). Taking dz quasi-static over the passage,

τj = τj(0) + (w̄ dz − g dz z̄/H)/v + (g dz/(vH)) zj   (8)

The middle term is a constant absorbed by the intercept of the least-squares fit A = [z, 1]. The last is proportional to zj and lands directly on the regression slope — the diagnostic quantity:

dτ/dz = (1/v)(1 + g dz/H)   (9)

So the gradient's first-order effect on the sign-carrying statistic is multiplicative, not additive. At g = 1, H = 20 cm and the largest injected amplitude dz = 3 cm, the slope is scaled by 1 ± 0.15. A sign flip needs g dz/H < −1, i.e. |dz| ≥ 20 cm — the full torso height. Equation (9) alone already says a quasi-static gradient cannot invert the diagnosis. The genuine worry was the dynamic version: with dz(t) oscillating at the breathing rate (draw_motion puts 0.30 Hz on the superior-inferior axis with a random phase), the surviving zero-mean linear term was expected to look like a phase-locked false travelling wave, mimicking retrograde flow in a healthy child.

4. Study 6 measured it, and refuted it

2160 trials: n_strip = 8, span 16 cm, SNR 60 dB, T = 20 frames, 36 trials × 4 classes × 5 amplitudes × 3 gradients. Marginals over gradient (metrics_motion2.json):

There is no penalty at any amplitude, and the full-gradient arm is nominally the best. The predicted phase-locked false travelling wave does not appear anywhere in the grid.

Do not read the table as evidence that the gradient helps. The three g arms at amplitude 0 are bit-identical cells (false-retrograde 0.1111, dir_acc 1.000, empty_lin 0.2939, empty_abstain 0.7083 in all three), because base_sigma multiplies w by shift, so g has no effect when shift = 0. One fifth of every marginal is literally the same 72 trials, and the remaining cells are seed-paired. An unpaired two-proportion test on 71.9% vs 79.4% (n = 360 each) returns z ≈ 2.3, but that test is invalid here and no multiplicity correction has been applied across the 15 cells. The defensible claim is “no measurable degradation”, not “improvement”.

5. Why the mechanism did not materialise — post hoc and untested

The following explanations were constructed after seeing the result, by reading the model. None has been tested. They are hypotheses, ranked by how directly the code supports them.

  1. The model's motion is separable, so its residual is standing, not travelling. In (6) the whole temporal degree of freedom is a single 3-vector disp[t]; the gradient modulates amplitude with z and never phase. To first order the no-bolus residual is δrj(t) ≈ ρ(zj) u(zj,t) = f(zj) dz(t) — rank one and multiplicative. Double centering leaves it (§1), but every row is then the same waveform up to a scalar. Cross-correlating rows of a rank-one separable matrix gives lag 0 for like-signed f, so tarr is constant, ss_tot ≈ 0, lin collapses to 0.0 by the explicit ss_tot > 1e-12 branch, and the trial abstains. Where f changes sign across the strip the rows anticorrelate, lags saturate at the window edge, tarr scatters, lin falls again, and the trial abstains. Both branches route to abstention, not to a false direction — which is exactly the measured failure signature.
  2. Equation (9) is sign-preserving. Even in the quasi-static limit where the gradient does reach the slope, it rescales rather than reverses it, by 15% at the extreme.
  3. Aperture fusion dilutes it. direction_features fuses G = 3 and G = 5 with weight base × (0.25 + q) × sqrt(dif_energy). A gradient artifact scales with the aperture's axial lever arm in the same way the real slope does, so it is not preferentially amplified in either arm.

Explanation 1, if correct, converts the refutation into a statement about the motion model rather than about the physics. Real respiratory mechanics is not separable: diaphragmatic excursion propagates caudally with a viscoelastic delay, so the displacement field is closer to u(z,t) = d(t − z/cprop) w(z) — a genuine travelling deformation with a z-dependent phase, which is the only form that can synthesize a coherent false arrival-time ramp. Nothing in this repository implements phase-lagged motion, and the negative Study 6 result therefore does not retire the risk it was built to test. The decisive experiment is one parameter: re-run the Study 6 grid with cprop swept over a range bracketing the bolus transit speed v, and report false-retrograde rate against v/cprop. Resonance at cprop ≈ v would be the failure the gradient arm was supposed to expose.

Amplitude, not gradient, is what hurts. At g = 1.0:

Raw accuracy halves while accuracy conditional on deciding falls only to 68%, and the difference is carried entirely by abstention. That is the clinically correct shape: the device degrades into “repeat the study”, not into a confident wrong direction, and the failure is self-announcing because the abstain rate is observable at the bedside without a ground truth. The mechanism is the wave-linearity gate: motion destroys the collinearity of tarr against z before it inverts its slope, so lin crosses below LIN_GATE earlier than the sign flips.

The residual to be honest about is the 11.1% false-retrograde floor at literally zero motion and zero gradient. Its arithmetic is visible: 70.8% of empty windows abstain, so 21 of 72 are decided, and 8 of those 21 (38%) are called retrograde — near coin-flip sign on windows containing no travelling event. The floor is therefore a property of the gate, not of motion: mean empty-window lin is 0.294 against a threshold of 0.35, so a substantial upper tail of noise-only windows clears it, and past the gate the sign is arbitrary. LIN_GATE = 0.35 is a single hand-set constant, Youden-selected once at 0.45 cm motion and never swept anywhere in the repository. The 11.1% is one unlabelled point on an ROC that has never been drawn; sweeping the gate over [0.2, 0.8] on the existing saved records would cost no new solves and would turn a floating number into a specificity/yield operating curve.

7. The common-mode estimate uses the zones that contain the signal

In _strip_aperture_stats the selector is sel = (world.zone_strip == strip) & (world.zone_m == m), and the mean in (2) is taken over exactly those zones. The common-mode reference is thus built from the same aperture, on the same strip, that the bolus is traversing. Substituting into (4), the surviving signal is

s̃j(t) = s(t − τj) − (1/N)Σk s(t − τk)   (10)

The subtrahend is s smeared by a discrete boxcar of width equal to the end-to-end transit time Δτ — a low-passed copy of the wanted signal. For the mid-strip zones, whose arrival time sits near the mean, (10) removes most of the excursion; the estimator is best conditioned at the ends of the strip and worst in the middle, which is also where the lever arm of the fit contributes least. At N = 5 zones (the G = 3 aperture in Study 6) the self-subtraction takes a full 20% of each zone's own amplitude, and it couples every pair of rows with weight −1/(N−1) — the induced correlation discussed in the noise section, here compounded by the fact that the removed part is signal rather than noise.

Meanwhile the device already carries a naturally signal-free reference: for unilateral reflux, the contralateral strip sees the same posture, the same breathing, the same electronics and no bolus. zone_strip exists, both strips are measured in every frame by measure_zones, and no code path anywhere in the repository forms a cross-strip common-mode estimate. The two strips are combined only at the very end, in decide_direction's max(… dif_energy) selector and in the energy_asym/lag_asym features.

The study that settles it is small and fully specified: replace the within-group mean in (2) with (a) the across-zone mean of the contralateral strip at the matched aperture, and (b) the leading principal component of the pooled two-strip zone matrix, then re-run the Study 6 grid at identical seeds and compare per-trial, paired, on abstain rate and on false-retrograde rate. Three arms are mandatory, not optional: unilateral reflux (where the contralateral reference is clean), bilateral reflux (where it is contaminated and the method should be expected to fail), and empty windows. Because dZ is already fractional, the known inter-strip electrode-area asymmetry — roughly 23% less realised area on one side, 25–57% higher |Z| — is largely normalised out, but a residual per-zone gain calibration must be fitted on the first two frames and reported, or the cross-strip estimate will import that asymmetry as a spurious common mode.

Scope. Every number here comes from one idealised cylinder (R = 5.5 cm, H = 20 cm, 6 rings, 17 layers) with literature tissue values and an assumed SNR budget; draw_anatomy varies organ positions only, so torso radius, fat thickness, organ sizes and all conductivities are identical across every “simulated child”. The mesh study additionally shows that absolute |Z| does not converge (11–76% error, oscillating) while the arrival-time slope does (6.80 → 0.11%), so nothing above should be read as an impedance claim — only as a claim about differential timing. No phantom and no patient data exist for any of it.

3.6  Estimation theory: arrival times, aperture fusion, and the abstain gate

The device claims a sign, produced by a specific chain of operations in _strip_aperture_stats, direction_features and decide_direction. Treated as an estimator, that chain is consistent in sign and inefficient in magnitude, and three of its constants have never been varied.

1. The estimand and the pipeline

Write the common-mode-rejected, frequency-averaged real series for zone j of one aperture as

ser[j,t] = c(t) + b(t − τj) + nj(t),   τj = zj / v   (1)

with b the passage waveform, c a zone-shared drift and v the signed axial bolus velocity. The estimand is neither v nor τ but sign(dτ/dz). Lags are never converted to seconds — xcorr_lag returns samples, slope is samples·cm−1, no frame rate appears in simulate_trial — which is discipline, not omission: a velocity claim would need the frame rate the sibling noise section shows to be implicit and unbudgeted, and a sign claim does not. Three stages follow — per-zone delay by cross-correlation against ser[0], least squares of tarr on zone_z, weighted fusion across apertures gated by fit quality — and each has a textbook optimal version the code does not implement.

2. Variance of a cross-correlation delay, and the bound

For a delay taken as the argmax of a correlation R(L), perturbing the stationarity condition R′(τ̂) = 0 to first order under additive noise gives

var(τ̂) ≈ k σeff2 / [ Eb |Rbb″(0)| ]   (2)

with Eb the passage energy and Rbb″(0) the curvature of the waveform autocorrelation at zero lag. The Cramér–Rao bound for time-delay estimation in white Gaussian noise has the same shape, var(τ̂) ≥ 1/(SNRout β2 TobsBw), β2 the mean-square bandwidth. Both say: precision buys down as 1/SNR and as the square of response sharpness. σeff is the post-processing noise the sibling section derives (its eq. 7), σeff ≤ σn√(1/F)√(1−1/n) = 0.632 σn for F = 2 and five zones.

Sharpness is where this device is structurally disadvantaged. bolus_path deliberately imposes no rise/fall envelope, so each zone's response is a smooth passage lasting a large fraction of the record: at the design configuration (metrics_design.json: n_strip 8, span 16.0, T 20) the grade-III bolus covers 12.24 cm in 19 intervals, 0.644 cm frame−1. Broad response, small β, so (2) is large in absolute terms. The estimator survives only by collapsing to a sign.

3. Two biases the implementation accepts

(a) Bartlett shrinkage toward zero lag. xcorr_lag divides every lag product by n, not the overlap n − |L|. The comment defends this correctly — the unbiased form lets noise win at the extremes — but the computed correlation is then the true one times a Bartlett taper w(L) = 1 − |L|/n, and a tapered peak moves toward zero by about

Δτ ≈ − [w′(τ)/w(τ)] · R(τ)/R″(τ)   (3)

strictly toward the origin, therefore sign-preserving but magnitude-attenuating. Every lag and slope magnitude here is biased low, by an amount growing with the lag measured — an independent reason no velocity or grade-from-magnitude claim is supported.

(b) Delay clipping at the useful lever arms. max_lag defaults to max(3, (2*n)//3), so T = 20 gives a ±13-sample window. At N = 8 over a 16 cm span the pitch is 2.286 cm and the widest usable aperture (G = 3) spans 9.14 cm of zone centroids — an end-to-end delay of 9.14/0.644 = 14.2 frames, outside the window, and at L = 13 the taper has already removed 65% of the correlation. The aperture with the best lever arm is the one whose delay is most clipped. The sign survives (the argmax saturates at the boundary with correct polarity) but the lever-arm benefit saturates rather than scaling.

This clipping analysis is derived from code constants and grade-III path geometry, not measured; it is worse for low grades, whose boluses are slower (GRADE_TABLE reach 0.20 at grade I). No run logs tarr against max_lag, so the saturation rate is unknown. One counter would fix that.

4. Is the estimator efficient? No, and what a matched filter would buy

All n delays are measured against a single reference, ser[0] (tarr[j], peaks[j] = xcorr_lag(ser[0], ser[j])). Every τ̂j therefore shares zone 0's noise, so cov(τ̂i, τ̂j) carries a common positive term and the residual covariance is dense with rank-one structure — on top of the −1/(n−1) correlation across-zone mean removal already induces. The efficient estimator under (1) is a matched filter: correlate each zone against the known passage template b(·), which the forward model generates exactly by sweeping the bolus along bolus_path with noise off. That would (i) remove the reference-noise coupling, (ii) attain the CRLB rather than approach it, and (iii) emit a likelihood ratio — a calibrated detection statistic that would replace the hand-set gate of §8 with a Neyman–Pearson threshold. It has not been tried; no code path builds a template. Likewise the discarded imaginary part: the quasi-static audit gives ωε/σ = 1.40 for kidney at 100 kHz, so Im(dZ) carries real information that zone_series drops for a defensible linearity reason, with no one measuring the Fisher information lost.

5. The slope fit: OLS where WLS is called for, and the leverage of a dead zone

The fit is plain unweighted least squares, coef = np.linalg.lstsq(np.vstack([zz, np.ones_like(zz)]).T, tarr). For equal-variance independent delays the slope standard error is

se(slope) = στ / √(Σj(zj − z̄)2) ≈ στ √12 / (B √n)   (4)

for n equally spaced zones spanning an axial baseline B: precision scales as B√n, the axial lever arm of the zone centroids, not electrode separation and not electrode count.

Equal weighting is wrong here in a way that is not merely inefficient. For low grades the zones above the bolus's reach see no signal — a grade-I event reaches only 0.20 of the bladder-to-kidney distance, so the superior half of a 16 cm strip regresses pure noise — and under OLS such a zone is not just uninformative but influential, sitting at the extreme of the regressor where leverage is highest:

6. The lever-arm identity, and the corrected electrode-count result

For N electrodes at pitch s, tetrapolar_zones drives (k, k+G) and senses (k+c, k+c+1) with c = (G−1)/2, so zone_z sits at (k + G/2)s and k runs 0 … N−G−1. Hence

aperture width A(G) = G s,   lever arm B(G) = (N−1−G) s,   A(G) + B(G) = (N−1)s = span   (5)

with zone count n(G) = N − G per strip. (5) is exact, and it is the central design constraint of the instrument: on a fixed span, every centimetre of aperture — that is, of depth reach — is paid for one-for-one out of the timing lever arm. Depth and timing precision are in strict zero-sum competition.

7. Aperture fusion: right variables, wrong exponents, never varied

The implemented weight is, verbatim, w = base * (0.25 + q) * np.sqrt(max(c["dif_energy"], 0.0)), with base = z_baseline and q = max(lin, 0) for n ≥ 3, else the literal 0.35.   (6)

Inverse-variance weighting of slope estimates, from (4), would instead use

wopt ∝ 1/se2 ∝ B2 · n · SNRzone   (7)

Comparing (6) with (7): the baseline exponent is 1 where theory says 2; the zone count n is absent; energy enters as √E where theory says E; and (0.25 + R2) is an affine rescaling with no derivation. The weight is monotone in the right three variables and wrong in every exponent. Plainly: the fusion weighting is heuristic, not derived from a noise model, and no variant of it has ever been run. The constants 0.25 and the 0.35 two-zone fallback appear once each in the repository and are swept nowhere.

The fused estimates are also strongly dependent — apertures share electrodes, adjacent zones share three of four — so independence-derived weights leave the mean unbiased but make any interval on the fused slope optimistic. None is quoted, which is right. Similarly ev = f["lag"] + f["slope"] adds two correlated estimators of the same quantity rather than averaging them: sign-correct, but ev is not a calibrated confidence and is not used as one.

8. The abstain gate is an operating point, chosen once

LIN_GATE = 0.35 is a single hand-set scalar, its provenance comment recording a Youden-optimal selection on measured lin distributions at 0.45 cm motion: 96.4% of real events kept, 71.4% of empty ones rejected, J = 0.678. Three things follow.

(a) Youden selection on evaluation data is optimistically biased. At a fixed threshold with N positives and N negatives, the SE of the empirical J is √[0.964·0.036/N + 0.714·0.286/N] = 0.035 at N = 200. Maximising over a threshold grid inflates reported J by about the expected maximum of that noise; neighbouring thresholds are highly correlated, so the effective number of independent tries is small (order 3–5) and E[max] ≈ 0.9–1.2 SE — an optimism of roughly 3–4 points of J, a few points of specificity at fixed sensitivity. Fixable at zero simulation cost: pick the gate on a held-out split of subjects and report on the remainder.

That optimism figure is an order-of-magnitude estimate: the sample size actually used to pick 0.35 is recorded in no metrics file, and the residuals entering lin are neither independent nor identically distributed. The claim to trust is not "3–4 J points" but "non-zero and currently unmeasured".

(b) One gate for every aperture size is indefensible. lin is the R2 of a two-parameter fit, so its null depends sharply on zone count: for n = 3 (one residual df) it is Beta(½,½), CDF (2/π)arcsin√x; for n = 5, Beta(½, 3/2), CDF (2/π)[arcsin√x + √(x(1−x))]. At 0.35:

A three-zone aperture fed pure noise clears the gate three times in five. One scalar threshold across zone counts is therefore an operating point whose false-pass rate varies twofold with geometry. A zone-count-dependent gate — equivalently a p-value or F-statistic in place of raw R2 — is the first thing to sweep.

Those null probabilities assume iid Gaussian residuals. The real residuals are correlated by across-zone mean removal (−1/(n−1) pairwise) and by the shared ser[0] reference, and the gated quantity is the fused weighted average of lin, for which no closed-form null exists. Right order of magnitude and right direction, not calibrated sizes; a permutation null over stored noise-only windows would give exact numbers.

(c) Two structural quirks. decide_direction tests f["lin"] — the fused, weight-averaged value — but conditions on f["n_zones"], which direction_features fills from best["n_zones"], the single aperture with the largest baseline×lin product: the quantity gated and the quantity deciding whether to gate come from different objects. And the guard reads if n_zones ≥ 3 and lin < lin_gate, so a two-zone aperture can never abstain — lin is 0.0 by construction at n = 2 and the gate is bypassed entirely, on precisely the series where common-mode rejection is structurally impossible. By (5) two-zone apertures arise only for odd N, so at the locked N = 8 the unguarded path is latent, not active; any move to an odd electrode count activates it.

And the gate cannot see the failure that matters most. At zero motion the abstain rate is 0.0% while false-retrograde sits at 11.1%: an unexplained floor, every call emitted with full confidence. lin is a coherence gate, not a correctness gate — a clean travelling wave whose estimated slope has the wrong sign passes perfectly, and nothing in decide_direction can detect that. Until the 11.1% is explained (leverage from a signal-free endpoint zone, §5, and boundary saturation of max_lag, §3b, are the testable candidates) the specificity ceiling of the method is set by an unidentified mechanism.

10. Assumed rather than tested, in this section

  • Untested: that cross-correlation against a single reference zone is adequate (a matched filter from a forward-model template would be efficient and supply a calibrated gate); that unweighted OLS on tarr is adequate (peaks[j] is already computed and discarded, and would serve as an inverse-variance weight).
  • Never varied: the fusion exponents in (6), against the theory-derived (7) and a uniform-weight control; and LIN_GATE, which was additionally Youden-selected on the data it was reported on.
  • Known false: that per-zone noise is white and iid across zones. Mean removal and the shared reference both induce correlation, and no GLS accounts for either.

None of these invalidate the sign. All say the same thing: a robust, deliberately conservative procedure operating some distance from its own bound, with the headroom between it and a properly derived estimator never measured.

3.7  Noise, instrumentation, and what the SNR budget assumes

Every other section of this report describes something the model computes. This section describes something the model asserts. The forward operator is verified to machine precision (V2, max relative reciprocity error 4.77×10−15 over 12 tetrapolar zones), the arrival-time slope converges monotonically across an 8× range of node count (V3: errors against the finest mesh of 6.80%, 2.84%, 0.97%, 0.11%), and the electrode boundary condition is a real Complete Electrode Model with finite contact impedance whose rejection factor has now been measured (V4). None of that says anything about whether a buildable instrument can resolve the quantity the estimator reads. That question is answered in the code by exactly one scalar, snr_db, whose default value is 60.0 in simulate_trial and which was chosen by the authors rather than read off a datasheet. It is the single most load-bearing unvalidated input in the project, and this section states precisely what it buys, what it hides, and what would falsify it.

A claim this report has made twice and must withdraw. Earlier drafts wrote that "the decision is invariant across an 8× range of mesh density". The V3 record does not support that. v3_mesh_convergence solves one deterministic, noiseless trial (_noiseless_trial(w, "reflux", grade 4, side +1, seed 4242)) at each of five refinement levels and records decision = 1, strip = 0 and lin of 0.984 to 0.997 at every level. Five identical decisions on one trial whose wave linearity is 0.98–1.00 is a consistency check with n = 1 and essentially no opportunity to fail; it is not an invariance result. What V3 does establish, and establishes well, is the monotone convergence of the fitted slope (1.536, 1.479, 1.452, 1.437, 1.438 samples·cm−1). Decision invariance would require the refinement sequence to be run over an ensemble of trials spanning the full range of lin, which has not been done; it is item 6 in section 11.

1. What the model actually injects

Noise enters at one place only, the last four statements of simulate_trial (directional_sim.py:317–321). The measured tensor Z of shape (T, F, n_zones) is first referenced to a resting baseline and normalised:

Zref = mean(Z[0:2])     dZ = (Z − Zref) / |Zref|   (1)

and then perturbed by a two-component complex noise:

σn = 10−SNR/20   (2)

dẐ[t,f,j] = dZ[t,f,j] + nind[t,f,j] + 0.5·ncor[t,f]   (3)

where the real and imaginary parts of both nind (shape (T,F,n_zones), drawn independently for every zone) and ncor (shape (T,F,1), broadcast identically across zones) are i.i.d. Gaussian with standard deviation σn. Four properties of this construction matter and should be stated plainly rather than left for a reviewer to discover.

  • The noise is fractional, and deliberately so. Because dZ is already a dimensionless fractional change, adding a fractional perturbation is the only unit-consistent choice. The in-code comment records that an earlier version derived σ from the raw |Z| and added it to the normalised quantity, which made the injected noise depend on the choice of length unit and left it sitting roughly 10 dB below the stated SNR. That was one of the fifteen defects found and fixed. The consequence is that SNR in this project means signal-to-noise on the normalised transfer impedance, not on a voltage, not on a raw impedance, and not on a reconstructed image. Section 5 shows that this choice also makes the implied absolute hardware requirement aperture-dependent, which the older version of this section hid inside an average.
  • The noise is added after normalisation, so the baseline is noiseless. Zref is computed from the clean solve. A real instrument divides by a noisy reference, which injects (i) a per-zone constant offset, correlated across every frame of that zone, and (ii) a per-zone multiplicative gain error. Both are second-order here and can be shown to be so: the offset is annihilated by the temporal mean removal ser − ser.mean(axis=1) at directional_sim.py:399, and the gain error is of size σn/√2 ≈ 7×10−4 at 60 dB (two averaged reference frames, real part only), which perturbs the per-zone weighting of the arrival-time fit by well under a tenth of a percent. This is a simplification we checked rather than one we are hoping is harmless, but it has not been simulated.
  • The zone-correlated component is exactly cancellable — for apertures with at least three zones, which is not every aperture the project sweeps. ncor has a trailing dimension of 1, so at a given (t, f) it is identical for every zone on both strips. _strip_aperture_stats subtracts the across-zone mean, but the guard at line 397 is if nz ≥ 3, while the aperture is admitted at nz ≥ 2 (line 384). At the locked N = 8 the odd outer gaps are G ∈ {3, 5, 7} giving 5, 3 and 1 zones per strip; the one-zone G = 7 aperture returns None, so every surviving aperture has nz ≥ 3 and ncor is removed exactly. But run_directional.py sweeps COUNTS = [4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12], and at N = 5 the single aperture has exactly 2 zones: the mean subtraction is skipped, lin is hard-set to 0.0 (line 413), the aperture enters the fusion at the fallback quality q = 0.35 (line 468), and because decide_direction gates only when n_zones ≥ 3 the estimator at N = 5 always makes a call, ungated, on a statistic that still carries ncor in full. The correct scoped statement is: the correlated half of the noise model contributes nothing to any aperture with ≥ 3 zones, which at N ≥ 6 is every aperture that survives the nz ≥ 2 filter; at N = 5 it survives unrejected and unassessed.
    Even where the cancellation is exact it is a best case, and the real risk lives in its complement: correlated disturbances in a real system are not uniform across channels, because channels differ in electrode area, cable route, multiplexer path and demodulator gain. Any spatial structure in a correlated disturbance survives the mean subtraction, and if that structure happens to be monotone in z it is indistinguishable from a travelling wave. Section 9 argues that this is the most likely explanation for the measured zero-motion false-retrograde floor.
  • The noise is white in time and has no bandwidth, and the correction to that runs against the estimator, not with it. One independent draw per (frame, frequency, zone), with no 1/f component, no spectral shape and no dependence on integration time. Real front-end noise at 50–100 kHz after synchronous demodulation is dominated by 1/f in the demodulated baseband, which is exactly the band (fractions of a Hz to a few Hz) in which the bolus signal lives. Earlier drafts said white noise is "the optimistic case because white noise averages out of a lag estimate". That understates the problem and gets the failure mode wrong. The observation window is 20 frames (section 2). Over 20 samples a 1/f process is not distinguishable from a low-order trend, so each zone acquires its own slow drift; xcorr_lag cross-correlating two slowly-drifting series returns a lag set by the shapes of those drifts, not by any travelling signal. Because adjacent zones at G = 3 share three of their four electrodes (section 9), their front-end drifts are strongly positively correlated, so the resulting lag sequence varies smoothly along z rather than randomly. lin is the R2 of a straight line through arrival time versus z. A smooth lag sequence scores high. So 1/f noise does not merely degrade the timing estimate — it plausibly manufactures the linearity the abstain gate keys on, which is the one mechanism that would convert the design's safe failure mode (abstain) into an unsafe one (confident wrong call). This is an argued mechanism, not a measured one; it directly qualifies the survival table in section 10 and it is the strongest single reason to run item 3 in section 11.

2. The implicit frame rate, the dwell per measurement, and therefore the measurement bandwidth

The SNR figure is meaningless without an integration time, and the code never states one. It can be recovered only under an assumption the code does not assert. Both draw_motion (lines 198–199) and draw_contact_z (line 219) hardcode np.sin(2π × 0.30 × t / 4.0 + φ) with t = np.arange(T). Nothing in the source says that 0.30 is in hertz or that 4.0 is samples per second. If 0.30 denotes Hz, then 2π(0.30)Δt = 2π(0.30)/4 and

Δt = 0.25 s, frame rate = 4 Hz, and with T = 20 (used in run_directional.py, run_motion2.py and run_precision.py; the simulate_trial default is T = 16) an observation window of 5.0 s.   (4)

Two defects and one physiological mismatch, all of which this section owns.

  1. The respiratory frequency is a dead parameter. simulate_trial carries the keyword breath_hz=0.30 in its signature at directional_sim.py:266. A grep over the entire repository finds that identifier on exactly that one line: it is never read, never passed to draw_motion or draw_contact_z, and has no effect on any result in this report. The model has no configurable respiratory frequency. This is a sixteenth defect and it is reported here for the first time. It is cosmetic in the sense that no published number changes; it is not cosmetic in the sense that a reader — or an author — would reasonably believe the respiratory rate had been varied, and it has not been.
  2. (4) is an assumption, not a derivation. The alternative reading — that the code encodes 0.30 cycles per 4 samples at some unstated Δt — is equally consistent with the source. Under that reading the frame rate is a free design parameter that this project does not constrain at all, and every number in this section that depends on Δt is a worked example rather than a requirement.
  3. 0.30 Hz is 18 breaths per minute, an older-child or adult rate. The target population is roughly 1–5 years old, where quiet respiration is commonly 20–30 breaths per minute, i.e. 0.33–0.50 Hz. (This is a recollection of standard pediatric vital-sign ranges, not a citation; no reference was consulted.) If the intended rhythm is pediatric, then holding the code's 0.30·t/4 form fixed forces Δt = 0.25 × (0.30 / fresp), which is 0.15–0.23 s, a frame rate of 4.4–6.7 Hz and a window of 3.0–4.5 s. Section 5's bandwidth numbers move by up to 2.2 dB across that range. Alternatively, if 4 Hz is taken as a fixed design choice, then the model is simulating a slower respiration than the patients will present, which makes the motion confounder in Study 6 easier than reality at fixed excursion amplitude, because fewer respiratory cycles fall in the window. Neither reading is established and the project cannot currently distinguish them.

One consequence is worth carrying forward regardless of which reading is right: at 0.30 Hz a 5.0 s window contains only 1.5 respiratory cycles. That is far too few for the estimator to average the respiratory term away, which is a precondition for the mechanism proposed in section 9.

The dwell per measurement, not the frame period, is what sets the noise bandwidth, and the two differ by more than an order of magnitude here. At N = 8 the odd outer gaps G ∈ {3, 5, 7} give (8−3) + (8−5) + (8−7) = 9 zones per strip and 18 zones total — exactly the n_zones = 18 recorded at every level of the V3 refinement sequence. 3. What the estimator actually sees, and what the variance arithmetic hides

The estimator does not consume dZ directly. zone_series takes the real part and averages over frequency, ser = dZ.real.mean(axis=1); _strip_aperture_stats then subtracts the across-zone mean when nz ≥ 3 and the temporal mean unconditionally. Propagating (3) through those operations:

  • Taking the real part discards the imaginary noise, leaving standard deviation σn per component, not √2 σn.
  • Averaging over F frequencies divides the noise standard deviation by exactly √F, because the draws at the two frequencies are independent. It does not divide the SNR by √F, because the two frequencies do not carry the same signal. For two frequencies whose real signal components have correlation ρ and comparable magnitude, the SNR gain is √(1 + ρ), i.e. between 0 and 3.01 dB, reaching 3.01 dB only at ρ = 1. The physical inputs suggest ρ is close to 1 — Re(dZ) tracks σ, and metrics_verify.json records σmuscle moving only 0.3400 → 0.3441 S·m−1 between 50 and 100 kHz — but the permittivities move much further (εr for kidney 30000 → 27991, with ωε/σ going 0.759 → 1.399), so the complex response is not frequency-flat and ρ is not 1 by construction. The model does not log it. The √F term below is therefore an upper bound on the realised gain, not the realised gain.
  • Subtracting the across-zone mean of n zones removes ncor exactly and reduces the independent variance by (1 − 1/n).

So the effective per-zone, per-frame noise standard deviation on the quantity the arrival-time fit regresses is, at best,

σeff ≤ σn · √(1/F) · √(1 − 1/n)   (7)

For F = 2 and a five-zone aperture, σeff = 0.632 σn, i.e. 3.98 dB of apparent improvement; for F = 1 and five zones, 0.894, i.e. 0.97 dB. The nominal 60 dB is therefore about 64.0 dB at the point of use in Studies 5 and 6 and about 61.0 dB in Study 1, and the two differ by a study-dependent 3 dB that appears nowhere else in this report. "SNR = 60 dB" is a label on an injection point, not a property of the estimator's input.

(7) counts a benefit and omits the cost that comes with it, and the cost is the same mechanism this section relies on twice more. Subtracting the across-zone mean does not merely shrink the variance; it makes the residuals dependent. For zone-mean-removed Gaussian residuals,

var(ñi) = σ2(1 − 1/n),   cov(ñi, ñj) = −σ2/n for i ≠ j, giving a pairwise correlation of exactly −1/(n−1): −0.25 for a five-zone aperture and −0.50 for a three-zone one.

_strip_aperture_stats then cross-correlates ser[0] against every ser[j]. A shared negative component contributes a negative correlation at lag zero for every pair. Since xcorr_lag selects argmax(c) and not argmax(|c|), a negative lag-zero contribution does not merely reduce the peak — it can push the selected peak away from zero entirely. That is structurally the same anti-phase mechanism invoked in section 9 to explain the empty-window false-positive floor. The section cannot claim the variance benefit of (7) and disown the correlation it creates: they are one operation. The honest position is that mean subtraction is still clearly correct (without it, common-mode terms dominate the lag-zero peak and the travelling term is invisible), that its variance benefit is real but small, and that it injects a structured negative coupling of magnitude 1/(n−1) whose consequence for xcorr_lag has never been measured. Measuring it is item 3 in section 11 and costs one line of code.

4. Unit consistency of the mixed cm / (S·m−1) system

Turning a fractional noise floor into an instrument specification requires reading the model's transfer impedance in ohms, and reading z0 in Ω·cm2. These are one computation, not two, and an earlier draft of this section made the mistake of settling the first in one subsection and then "discovering" a 100× problem with the second several subsections later. The system assembled in CEM3D._assemble is a single matrix mixing three blocks, and consistency has to hold across all three or none.

  • Stiffness. ∫σ∇u·∇v dV with mesh coordinates in centimetres and admittivities in S·m−1. The integral carries one net power of length, so Knum = σSI Lcm = 100 × (σL)SI = 100 KSI.
  • Electrode conductance. Cdiag[l] = self.area[l] / zc[l] (eit3d.py:129) with area accumulated from boundary facets in cm2, so areanum = 104 areaSI.
  • Coupling block. Bcols[:, l] = −(self._c[l] / zc[l]), whose numerator is the facet row-sum of the same electrode mass matrix and therefore scales identically to area.

For the assembled system to be a consistent non-dimensionalisation, the contact blocks must carry the same factor 100 as the stiffness: 104 areaSI / znum = 100 × areaSI / zSI, hence

Zphys = 100 × Zmodel  and  zphys = znum / 100 Ω·m2 = 100 × znum Ω·cm2   (8)

Both halves of (8) follow from the same requirement and stand or fall together. The drawn range z0 ~ U(5, 20), labelled Ω·cm2, is therefore approximately 500–2000 Ω·cm2.

The audit invalidates the code's stated physical rationale, not merely a label, and that is the more serious finding. draw_contact_z's docstring argues: "the meaningful quantity is the dimensionless group zσ/L … with σ ~ 0.34 and a mesh in cm, that group reaches unity near z ~ 3, and real skin-electrode impedance is comparable to or larger than the tissue spreading resistance, so z0 of order 5-20 is the physical regime." The first clause is correct and unaffected: the group zphysσ/L = znumσ/Lcm ∈ [1.7, 6.8] for σ = 0.34 and L = 1 cm is invariant under (8), so the physics of the V4 sweep is well posed and no simulated result changes. But the second clause reasons in numeric units about a physical regime, and under (8) it is in fact arguing for 500–2000 Ω·cm2. That is a dry or textile electrode regime, not a wet gel one. Any statement in this project about acceptable skin preparation, or about which electrode technology the SNR budget assumes, that was derived from the "5–20" label is wrong.

Two things must be said and neither is resolved. First, this is an arithmetic audit performed for this section, not a measured result; it should be independently checked by dimensional analysis of _assemble before it is relied on. Second, it is tempting to conclude that a gel-electrode build would do better than V4's measured rejection because z would be smaller, and that conclusion is not supported: draw_contact_z's own docstring warns that values ~100× smaller make the electrodes near-ideal shunts, suppressing the bolus signal while maximising sensitivity to contact drift, and V4's full sweep (which includes z0 = 1) does not resolve monotonicity because it reports only a peak-to-peak spread. The direction of error under a change of electrode technology is unknown. A bench measurement of specific contact impedance at 50–100 kHz for the intended electrode is the only thing that settles it.

6. Is 60 dB reachable? Yes — but not for the reason, or from the source impedance, the earlier draft claimed

Thermal noise at a differential input is set by the real part of the source impedance seen by that input. The transfer impedance is not that quantity. Z here is a four-terminal ratio Vsense/Idrive between two disjoint electrode pairs; it is not a resistance in the fluctuation–dissipation sense and it is not what the amplifier looks into. An earlier draft computed √(4kTR) with R = 26.9 Ω and reported 0.68 nV/√Hz. That is the wrong physics and it is retracted.

The correct source impedance is the two sense electrode–skin interfaces in series with the local spreading resistance. From the §4 audit the specific contact impedance is 500–2000 Ω·cm2. The nominal electrode patch in place_strips is 2 × arc_half × R = 2(0.30)(5.5) = 3.3 cm circumferentially by 2 × 0.40 × pitch axially (1.83 cm at a 16 cm span, 1.37 cm at 12 cm), giving a nominal 4.5–6.0 cm2; the realised area is smaller because it snaps to boundary facets. Spreading resistance for a ~1.3 cm-radius patch on σ ≈ 0.34 S·m−1 is 1/(4σa) ≈ 58 Ω per electrode. So

Re{Zsource} ≈ 2 × (zphys/A + Rspread) ≈ 0.2 – 1.2 kΩ, taken as 1 kΩ below   (10)

with the upper end reached if the realised area is small or the specific contact impedance is at the top of the audited range. Two qualifications, both of which cut in the design's favour and neither of which is measured: at 50–100 kHz the electrode interface is substantially capacitive, and Johnson noise depends on the real part only, so (10) overstates the thermal term; and the drive-side contact impedance does not appear at the sense input at all.

The honest reading of those numbers is not "60 dB is easy". It is that 60 dB is not an electronic-noise specification at all. Nothing about resolving 1000 ppm on a 10–46 Ω transfer impedance with a 6.9 ms dwell is hard; lock-in demodulation of a 50 kHz carrier over 347 cycles is a textbook measurement. What is hard, and what 60 dB is silently standing in for, is everything that is not stationary white noise: electrode artefact, motion, drift, cardiac pulsatility, interference and systematic error. Section 8 lists what the model omits from that category. The figure 60 dB happens to sit in the range research EIT systems typically quote as an end-to-end system SNR including artefact, which is why it is not an unreasonable placeholder — but that is a recollection of the literature, not a citation, and no datasheet was consulted at any point in this project. The model's noise is the easy kind, injected at a level plausible for the hard kind.

Note also the term the earlier draft omitted entirely and which dominates once the source impedance is kilohms rather than tens of ohms: amplifier input current noise. At 26.9 Ω it is irrelevant; at 1–2 kΩ a 5 pA/√Hz part would contribute 5–10 nV/√Hz and become co-dominant with the voltage-noise term. For a dry or textile electrode front end — which §4 says is the regime this model is actually in — input current noise at 100 kHz is the specification that matters, and this project has never named it. It is item 8 in section 11.

The drive amplitude is a free parameter with exactly zero modelled effect. measure_zones forms (U[p] − U[q]) / amp while solve_drives builds a right-hand side proportional to amp; the system is linear, so amp cancels identically and amp = 1.0 is never varied in any run script. Consequently the project places no constraint whatsoever on injected current, and therefore none on patient auxiliary current, electrode current density, or the current-versus-SNR trade that dominates real bioimpedance front-end design. Every microvolt figure in this section scales linearly with the assumed 1 mA and is otherwise unconstrained. The usual regulatory ceiling on patient auxiliary current at 50 kHz is well above 1 mA, so we do not expect this to bind, but that statement is a recollection of IEC 60601-1's frequency-scaled limit and has not been checked against the standard, and pediatric application may carry additional constraints this project has not investigated. Anyone sizing hardware from this report must derive the current budget independently.

7. Contact impedance: correcting an overclaim, and stating what V4 does not measure

The contact impedance is not a constant. draw_contact_z draws a per-electrode base z0 ~ U(5, 20) and modulates it in time:

zl(t) = z0l · clip[ (1 + (b(t) + d(t))(0.5 + 0.5 cosφl)) · (1 + 0.03·A·ξl(t)), 0.7, 1.4 ]   (11)

with b(t) = 0.03 sin(2π(0.30)t/4 + ψ) a respiratory modulation, d(t) a linear drift ramping to an N(0, 0.03) endpoint, φl ~ U(0, 2π) an independent per-electrode phase, and ξ an independent Gaussian term scaling with the motion amplitude A. Two features matter. First, the drift and respiratory terms are not scaled by A — they are present even at motion_amp = 0. Second, the modulation depth gl = 0.5 + 0.5cosφl is random per electrode, so the disturbance is spatially structured along the strip.

The report elsewhere states that contact impedance "largely drops out" of a tetrapolar measurement, and strip3d.py's module docstring says "contact impedance largely rejected". V4 shows this is an overclaim and it must be corrected. Sweeping a uniform z0 and comparing the tetrapolar transfer impedance against a like-for-like bipolar proxy (the driven pair's own potential difference, same electrodes, same solve):

The correct statement is: tetrapolar sensing reduces contact-impedance sensitivity by about a factor of four over the modelled range; it does not eliminate it. Expressed as a log-sensitivity over the 4:1 sweep,

a ≡ ∂ln|Ztetra| / ∂ln z ≈ 0.1621 / ln 4 = 0.117   (12)

which is the number section 9 needs. The mechanism is standard and worth stating for the device engineer: four-wire sensing rejects contact impedance only to the extent that the sense electrodes draw no current, and in the CEM they draw a little, through the Robin term u + zlσ∂u/∂n = Ul assembled as Ml/zl in CEM3D._assemble. The residual scales with the finite input impedance of the sense path, which in the model is set by the electrode geometry and in hardware is set by the amplifier and the cable. A front end whose differential input impedance at 100 kHz is not enormously larger than the electrode impedance will do worse than 4.34×, not better. The design needs a stated input-impedance specification and does not have one.

Two limits of V4 that must be stated before its number is reused.

  1. V4 sweeps a uniform contact impedance (zc = np.full(world.L, z0)), so 0.1621 is the common-mode contact sensitivity. The disturbance that (11) actually injects is per-electrode and spatially structured, and the quantity that governs its effect on the estimator is the differential sensitivity — how much a zone's transfer impedance responds when one of its four electrodes moves and the others do not. No verification in this project measures that. The first-order estimates in section 9 apply (12), a common-mode log-sensitivity, to a differential perturbation, which is not strictly licensed. They establish an order of magnitude and nothing more. A V5 sweeping one electrode at a time would cost a handful of solves and would close this.
  2. V4 covers 8 of 18 zones: strip 0 only, and only the G = 3 and G = 5 apertures. The widest aperture and the entire contralateral strip are untested for contact rejection.

The following are absent from the model entirely, ranked by expected threat to a direction-from-timing estimator. Each is listed with the mechanism by which it would reach the estimator, because "not modelled" is only useful if it comes with a reason to care.

Cardiac-synchronous impedance pulsatility Blood volume and velocity modulate tissue admittivity at the heart rate. In a 1–5 year old that is roughly 1.5–2.5 Hz at rest. This is the entire physical basis of impedance cardiography and impedance plethysmography, where thoracic ΔZ/Z of order 0.1–1% is routinely measured. (ii) Aliasing is the real threat. A 1.5–2.5 Hz component sampled at 4 Hz sits at or above Nyquist; 2.5 Hz folds to 1.5 Hz, and because heart rate wanders (respiratory sinus arrhythmia, crying, bladder filling discomfort) the aliased component drifts slowly and lands squarely in the 0–2 Hz band the bolus occupies. The most dangerous omission after cardiac. It is motion-correlated, broadband, and per-channel, so it is neither removed by the across-zone mean subtraction nor independent of the confounder the design is already fighting. A cable that moves with respiration produces a per-zone disturbance phase-locked to breathing and spatially structured — the signature of a travelling wave. Study 6 shows the estimator degrades with motion amplitude independent of gradient; this mechanism is amplitude-scaled and would compound that.

At 50–100 kHz it is out of band and the demodulator rejects it, so the first-order threat is input headroom, not signal. The second-order threat is real: after any AC-coupling network, a step in half-cell potential (a child shifting position) produces a settling transient in the demodulated baseband — the same sub-hertz band as the bolus — and settling transients are monotone in time, which is precisely what the arrival-time regression is looking for.
Body-as-antenna common-mode pickup The body couples capacitively to mains wiring; common-mode voltage converts to differential through finite CMRR and, dominantly, through the potential-divider effect of mismatched electrode impedances. The model contains no common-mode source at all, so although it draws electrode impedances independently over a 4:1 range (a large real-world mismatch), it cannot express the consequence of that mismatch for interference rejection. CMRR degrades with frequency, and 100 kHz is the worse of the two bands used. Section 4's audit makes this sharper: at 500–2000 Ω·cm2 the electrode impedances are high, and high source impedance with 4:1 mismatch is the classic worst case for common-mode-to-differential conversion.

See the dedicated treatment below; the earlier draft quantified this from the wrong quantity and the number it printed is withdrawn.
Mains interference (50/60 Hz and harmonics) Out of band relative to the carrier and largely removed by synchronous demodulation, but it consumes input range, intermodulates, and can alias. The specific risk is aliasing. Any residual mains ripple surviving the demodulator's low-pass folds into the 0–2 Hz band on sampling, and 50 Hz sampled at 4 Hz lands at DC — indistinguishable from drift. Note this is a smaller version of the cardiac problem above: mains is at least at a known, stable frequency that a notch can track, whereas the cardiac component wanders.
ADC quantisation, channel gain mismatch, multiplexer crosstalk, switching settling Ordinary instrumentation non-idealities. Mostly benign for a timing estimator, with one exception: settling after a drive electrode is switched to sense duty is a known EIT artefact, and the model solves an instantaneous steady state per drive, so it cannot represent it at all. The 6.9 ms dwell of section 2 is the budget it would have to fit in, and at 1 kΩ source impedance that is a real constraint on multiplexer and cable capacitance.

Demodulator phase error, done correctly. An earlier draft computed the leakage from the standing impedance: from V2's recorded example Z = 0.3246 − 0.0506j, giving |Im/Re| = 0.1558, and concluded that a 1° phase error is "2.7× the entire 60 dB noise budget". Both halves of that are wrong and are withdrawn. The estimator does not consume Z; it consumes dZ = (Z − Zref)/|Zref|, and |Zref| is a magnitude and therefore rotation-invariant, so

dZ′ = eiθ dZ exactly, and Re(dZ′) = Re(dZ)cosθ − Im(dZ)sinθ   (13)

The fractional error on the estimator's input is therefore r·tanθ with r = Im(dZ)/Re(dZ), the imaginary-to-real ratio of the bolus perturbation — not the 0.1558 of the standing impedance. The model never logs r. Nor can it be bounded from the tissue table: the perturbation is the difference between urine (ωε/σ = 1.31×10−4 at 50 kHz, essentially purely resistive) and the tissue it displaces, which at 50–100 kHz is not resistive at all (kidney ωε/σ = 0.759 and 1.399; muscle 0.213 and 0.392). r could plausibly lie anywhere between 10−4 and order unity and this project has not constrained it. Logging it costs one line and is item 4 in section 11.

Second, and independently of the missing ratio: the resulting error is proportional to |dZ|, so it is a multiplicative gain on the signal, whereas σn is an additive floor. Comparing the two as if they were commensurate ("2.7× the noise budget") is a category error. The argument that actually holds is this:

  • A static phase error acts as a per-zone multiplicative gain (cosθ − r sinθ). It perturbs the aperture weight w = z_baseline × (0.25 + q) × √(dif_energy) at directional_sim.py:469 and shifts the relative weighting of zones in the arrival-time fit, but it does not shift arrival times and therefore cannot change the sign of the slope. It is largely benign, which is what the earlier draft's own next sentence conceded while contradicting its headline number.
  • A drifting phase error is the threatening case, and it needs its own treatment rather than a reuse of the static number. Over the observation window it adds an in-band term of size Im(dZ) × Δθ, coherent, slow, and per-channel if each zone has its own demodulator. It is additive, it lives in the bolus band, and its spatial structure is set by hardware layout. Its magnitude cannot be computed here because Im(dZ) is not logged.
  • Whether it is rejected at all depends on an architecture choice the project has never made: if all zones are multiplexed through one demodulator the drift is common mode and the across-zone mean subtraction removes it; if each channel has its own, it is not removed. That is item 8 in section 11.

Note what is partially modelled and should not be double-counted: slow contact-impedance drift and respiratory contact modulation are present, via (11); rigid and non-rigid body motion are present, via draw_motion and the grad parameter, and were characterised in Study 6.

Motion cannot explain the floor: at motion_amp = 0, draw_motion returns an identically zero displacement field. Nor is the floor amplitude-driven — across the amplitude ladder at grad = 1.0 the false-retrograde rate goes 0.111, 0.194, 0.167, 0.167, 0.083, which is flat to declining with every interval overlapping every other. Whatever generates apparent travelling waves out of empty windows is present at rest.

Candidate mechanism. Even at zero motion, (11) applies a respiratory-plus-drift modulation h(t) = b(t) + d(t) to every electrode, weighted by a random per-electrode depth gl = 0.5 + 0.5cosφl. Its size can be estimated from (12): with |h| ≲ 0.06 combining the 3% respiratory term and the N(0, 0.03) drift endpoint, and a log-sensitivity of 0.117, the raw fractional excursion in |Z| from contact modulation alone is up to 0.117 × 0.5 × 0.06 ≈ 3.5×10−3 — roughly 3.5× the injected noise floor at 60 dB and about 5.5× σeff. That is the term the mean subtraction has to remove.

The correct algebra for what survives it is not the earlier draft's. There is no gj per zone: draw_contact_z draws gl per electrode (size = world.L), and a zone is four electrodes — drive (k, k+G) and sense (k+c, k+c+1) with c = (G−1)/2, from tetrapolar_zones. To first order

δln|Zj|(t) = h(t) · wj, with wj = Σl ∈ zone j sjl gl   (14)

where sjl = ∂ln|Zj|/∂ln zl are the four per-electrode log-sensitivities, whose sum V4 constrains to ≈ 0.117 but whose individual values no verification measures (section 7). The electrode sharing between zones is aperture-dependent and this matters:

  • G = 3 (c = 1): zone k uses {k, k+1, k+2, k+3} and zone k+1 uses {k+1, k+2, k+3, k+4} — three of four electrodes shared. Consecutive wj are strongly positively correlated, so the weight sequence along z is smooth, not sign-random.
  • G = 5 (c = 2): zone k uses {k, k+2, k+3, k+5} and zone k+1 uses {k+1, k+3, k+4, k+6} — only one of four shared, so this aperture's weights are much closer to independent.

This cuts in the hypothesis's favour and the earlier draft missed it. The G = 3 aperture supplies 5 of the 8 usable zones at N = 8 and carries the longest lever arm, and its smooth weight sequence makes a monotone ramp in z — and therefore a high lin and a gate pass — substantially more likely than an independent-sign picture would suggest.

The rest of the mechanism runs as follows. (14) is a separable, rank-one spatiotemporal perturbation: every zone sees the same time course, scaled differently. Separable perturbations should carry no lag information — exactly the argument bolus_path makes for why the bolus must not be amplitude-modulated. The estimator breaks that argument in two places. First, after across-zone mean subtraction zone j's residual is (wj − w̄)h(t), whose weight can be negative. Second, xcorr_lag takes argmax(c), not argmax(|c|). For a zone whose residual weight has the opposite sign to the reference zone, the correlation is negative near lag zero and the argmax is pushed out to a large, structured lag. Because wj is smooth in z at G = 3, the sign-flipped zones form a contiguous block at one end of the strip, so the resulting arrival-time sequence is a step or ramp along z, scores a high lin, passes the gate, and returns a confident direction. Note that h(t) over a 5.0 s window at 0.30 Hz completes only 1.5 cycles and is superposed on a monotone drift, so it is close to a one-sided ramp — which makes it more ramp-like, not less.

This mechanism is a hypothesis constructed by reading the code and applying a common-mode sensitivity to a differential perturbation. It is not measured, and V4 does not license the second step (section 7). It is consistent with three independent observations — the measured empty-window lin of 0.294 sitting just below the 0.35 gate, the 3.5×-noise-floor size estimate above, and the smoothness argument from electrode sharing — but consistency is not demonstration. Four cheap experiments would settle it, none requiring new physics:

  1. Rerun empty windows with the respiratory and drift terms of (11) disabled (b(t) = d(t) = 0). If the floor collapses, contact modulation is the source; if it survives, it is the gate and the finite window length.
  2. Log the realised per-zone weight wj and its Spearman rank correlation with z, per trial, on the empty windows that leak. The prediction is that leaking windows have |rank correlation| near 1 and rejected windows do not. This directly tests the smoothness argument and distinguishes it from a pure-chance gate leak.
  3. Replace argmax(c) with a sign-tracking argmax(|c|) in xcorr_lag and re-measure. This tests the anti-phase step specifically, and it is the same code change that would test the −1/(n−1) coupling identified in section 3.
  4. Add a per-electrode V5 to the verification suite that perturbs one zl at a time and reports the 4×18 sensitivity matrix sjl. Without it, (14) has an unmeasured coefficient.

Until those are run, the honest position is that an 11.1% [5.7%, 20.4%] intrinsic false-positive floor exists, is not explained by motion, is not amplitude-driven, shows no detectable sign bias at n = 21, and is not fully explained.

11. The cheapest experiments that would retire this assumption

  1. Re-seed the noise independently of the anatomy, then extend and widen the SNR sweep. The current sweep amplitude-scales one realisation per anatomy and therefore underestimates decision variance. Draw the noise from a separate generator, add 30, 35, 40 and 45 dB, use n ≥ 300 per cell rather than 32, and report the per-trial flip rate alongside aggregate accuracy. Then repeat it inside Studies 5 and 6 rather than only Study 1. Pure compute; no new physics, hardware or modelling. This converts the largest unvalidated input in the project from an assertion into a characterised operating range, and it is the single highest-value item on this list.
  2. Report signal, not just noise. The model never records the per-zone peak bolus-induced fractional excursion maxt|Re dZ[t, j]|. Without it, "SNR = 60 dB" is a statement about the denominator with the numerator unstated, and the true measurement signal-to-noise ratio is unknown. Logging it costs nothing and is the single most informative number missing from this report. Section 9's estimate that contact modulation alone reaches 3.5× the noise floor is only interpretable against it.
  3. Add a coloured-noise arm. Inject 1/f alongside the white term and re-measure the abstain rate and lin distribution on empty and travelling windows. Section 1 argues that 1/f inflates lin rather than degrading it, which would invert the design's safe failure mode. That argument is the most consequential unmeasured claim in this section. The same run should test the argmax(|c|) change to xcorr_lag, since the two interact.
  4. Log the three ratios the estimator's error analysis needs and does not have: Im(dZ)/Re(dZ) per zone (sets the demodulator phase-error sensitivity, §8); the coherence of Re(dZ) between 50 and 100 kHz (sets whether the √F gain in (7) is 3 dB or 0 dB); and the realised per-zone contact weight wj with its rank correlation against z (tests §9's mechanism). All three are one-line additions to simulate_trial or direction_features.
  5. Add V5, a per-electrode contact sensitivity check. Perturb one zl at a time and report the full sensitivity matrix sjl. V4 measures only the common-mode response to a uniform z0, over 8 of 18 zones, on one strip; the disturbance the model injects is per-electrode and the mechanism in §9 depends on the differential response. This is the missing verification.
  6. Rerun the mesh refinement over an ensemble. V3's decision stability rests on one trial with lin ≥ 0.98. Running the five-level sequence over, say, 32 trials spanning the lin distribution would convert "the slope converges" into the decision-level claim this report has twice tried to make and cannot currently support.
  7. Measure a phantom. A saline-filled phantom with the intended electrode array, driven by the intended front end, gives simultaneously: a real absolute |Z| per aperture (which V3 says the model cannot supply), a real noise spectrum including 1/f, a real specific contact impedance at 50–100 kHz, a real source impedance, and a real CMRR figure. That single bench experiment would retire (8), (9), the σn assumption, the z0 unit audit of §4 and most of the "not modelled" table at once. No amount of further simulation substitutes for it.
  8. Specify the four hardware parameters the design silently depends on: (i) the differential input impedance of the sense path at 100 kHz, which sets whether the measured 4.34× contact rejection is achievable or optimistic; (ii) the amplifier input current noise at 50–100 kHz, which is the dominant term once the source impedance is kilohms and which §4's audit says it is; (iii) whether zones share one demodulator or have their own, which sets whether phase drift is common mode and removable; and (iv) the acquisition order and dwell schedule, which sets the multiplexer skew of §2 and the settling budget of §8.
  9. Decide what the frame rate is. Fix the dead breath_hz parameter so respiration is configurable, state Δt explicitly rather than leaving it to be inferred from a hardcoded constant, and re-run Study 6 at a pediatric respiratory rate of 0.33–0.50 Hz. At present the project cannot say whether it is simulating a 4 Hz device breathing at 18/min or a 6.7 Hz device breathing at 30/min, and the two imply different dwell budgets and different numbers of respiratory cycles inside the observation window.

Until at least the first three are done, the correct summary of this section is short: the noise model is internally consistent, unit-correct once (8) is applied, and entirely assumed. Its numerical value is comfortably achievable by real electronics — roughly 40 dB of electronic-noise margin at the binding zone, on corrected arithmetic. Its character — white, stationary, bandwidth-free, half of it exactly cancellable, sampled with one realisation per anatomy — is the optimistic case in every dimension that matters, and the disturbances most likely to defeat a direction-from-timing estimator (cardiac pulsatility, 1/f drift, cable artefact, per-channel phase drift) are precisely the ones it does not contain.

3.8  Threats to validity

Everything below is a reason to disbelieve a number in this report. The section is ordered from the threat that is structural and unfixable within a simulation, to the ones that are arithmetic and fixable in an afternoon. Two of them — the prevalence model and the base rate — change the interpretation of headline figures that are otherwise correctly computed.

1. Inverse crime: weaker here than usual, but not absent

Data are generated and analysed inside one forward model. In model-based EIT inversion this is the classic inverse crime: the reconstruction operator is built from the same discretised operator K that produced the data, so discretisation error cancels and reported image quality is unattainable on real measurements. That specific mechanism does not operate here, because the estimator never touches the forward operator. It consumes only the per-zone series and fits

(1)   tj = a + b zj  over zones j,  decision = sign(b + lag),  gated on wave linearity ≥ LIN_GATE

where zj is the axial centroid of the tetrapolar zone and tj its arrival time. Nothing in direction_features, xcorr_lag or decide_direction references the mesh, the CEM coupling blocks or the admittivity field. So the estimator inherits none of the FEM's discretisation error, which is exactly why the mesh study found the decision invariant across all five meshes while |Z| itself did not converge.

The residual bias is different and it is the serious one: the estimator's assumed response shape is guaranteed to match the simulated response shape. Equation (1) presumes each zone sees a single-peaked perturbation whose time of maximum tracks the bolus centroid past that zone. bolus_path produces precisely that, and says so — its docstring states that the bolus is a slug of essentially constant volume that moves, and that a rise/fall envelope was deliberately not imposed because an envelope makes the response separable as (amplitude per zone) × (one shared time course), and a separable response has no meaningful relative lag. That is a correct statement of physics and simultaneously an admission: the one modelling choice that would break the estimator was excluded from the simulator by design. Real ureteric peristalsis is a bolus that forms, propagates and is squeezed, i.e. partly separable. The fraction of a real per-zone signal that is separable envelope rather than travelling centroid is the unmeasured quantity in this project, and no run in the repository bounds it.

Reciprocity at 4.77e−15 and slope convergence to 0.11% are internal-consistency checks. Neither is evidence against inverse crime: both would pass identically if the simulated response shape were physically wrong, because both compare the model to itself. The test that would bear on it is external — a flow phantom with a peristaltic pump, where the per-zone waveform is measured rather than assumed, and the separable fraction can be estimated by fitting a rank-1 (amplitude × envelope) model and reporting the residual.

2. Model error the forward solver cannot see

  • Geometry. A right circular cylinder, R = 5.5 cm, height 20 cm, 6 rings, 17 layers. No spine, no psoas, no iliac crest, no ribs, no lordosis. The flank strips sit on a surface of constant curvature; a real flank is not one.
  • No bowel gas. TISSUE contains muscle, fat, kidney, bladder wall and urine. There is no air entry and base_sigma assigns none. Gas is a near-zero-conductivity inclusion that moves on its own schedule in the anterior and medial abdomen. It is the most obvious source of a spurious travelling perturbation with no relation to the ureter, and it is entirely absent. Study 6 tested body motion, which is a different confounder: motion displaces the whole conductivity field coherently, gas changes its topology locally.
  • Adiposity. Fat is present, as a shell at r > 0.82 R — a thickness of 0.18 × 5.5 = 0.99 cm in every simulated child, with no variation anywhere in the codebase. Since the strips are surface-mounted and the sensitivity of a tetrapolar pair falls steeply with depth, subcutaneous thickness is plausibly the single largest amplitude modulator in a real cohort, and it is held fixed.
  • Tissue values. Literature conductivities with a two-parameter dispersion, σ(f) = σ0(1 + 0.04 log10(f/50 kHz)) and εr(f) = εr0(50 kHz/f)0.10. This is a fit, not a Cole–Cole model, and pediatric tissue is not adult tissue. It matters more than usual because ωε/σ = 1.40 for kidney at 100 kHz: the permittivity term is load-bearing, so an error in εr0 is an error in the signal, not a rounding detail.
  • Urine conductivity is a constant. TISSUE["urine"] = 1.70 S/m, identical in every trial. Real urine spans roughly 0.5–3 S/m with hydration and osmolality, and a child undergoing a voiding study is deliberately hydrated, i.e. at the dilute end. The bolus signal scales with the urine–muscle contrast, 1.70 − 0.34 = 1.36 S/m as modelled; at 0.8 S/m urine the contrast falls to 0.46 S/m, a factor of ~3 in perturbation amplitude. Study 6 showed the failure mode is amplitude-driven and safe (abstention rises), so the expected consequence is a higher abstain rate rather than a wrong sign — but that is an inference from a different experiment, not a measurement. A one-line sweep of TISSUE["urine"] over 0.6–2.5 S/m would settle it and has not been run.

The 48 children are 48 draws of organ position inside one fixed body. That is a legitimate and useful ensemble — positional uncertainty is exactly what a placement study needs to marginalise over — but it means "cohort" here denotes a positional-jitter ensemble and nothing else. No result in this repository supports any statement about body habitus, age range, BMI, or scaling from infant to adolescent. In particular the Study 5 recommendation mixes parameterisations: the span is absolute (10.0 cm) while the centre is fractional (z_center = 0.34). On a torso half as long, a 10 cm strip covers twice the fractional height, and nothing here tests which of the two conventions transfers. The honest version of the recommendation is "10 cm at z = 0.34 on a 20 cm torso"; whether it generalises is an open question requiring a torso-size sweep that the code already supports (R, height and xscale are constructor arguments) and that has not been run.

4. Thresholds selected on the data they are reported on

There is no train/test split anywhere in this project. Two selections are made on the evaluation set:

  • LIN_GATE = 0.35 is a single hand-set module-level constant. Its docstring records a Youden justification from measured lin distributions at 0.45 cm motion (events median ~0.95, empty windows ~0.12–0.25; keeps 96.4% of events, rejects 71.4% of empties), which is a real calibration and a large improvement on the earlier hand-picked 0.55. But it is never swept anywhere in the repository, so no sensitivity of any reported figure to this constant exists. Every abstain rate quoted — 5.6% at 1 cm motion, 30.6% at 2–3 cm — is a function of one untuned number.
  • best_k in subject_analysis is chosen as argmaxk of Youden's J over the six candidate rules (flag if ≥ k of K = 6 events read retrograde), and best_sens / best_spec are then reported at that k, on the same 24 refluxing and 24 healthy children that chose it. With six candidates and n = 24 per arm, the winner's J is upward-biased by selection. Evidence that this is not hypothetical: the chosen k moves across cells — 3, 3, 4, 4 for the 10 cm arm at σ = 0/0.5/1/2 cm, and 1, 1, 1, 1 for the 16 cm arm — which is a threshold tracking noise as much as signal. The fix is standard: pre-register a rule (a simple majority, k ≥ 4) or select k on a disjoint set of children and report on the rest.

5. A prevalence model that is published but never called

GRADE_WEIGHTS = {1: 0.30, 2: 0.30, 3: 0.22, 4: 0.13, 5: 0.05} is defined at directional_sim.py:51 and rendered into the methods table by build_methods.py:76 as the project's grade model. sample_grade(rng) is defined at directional_sim.py:62 and, by exhaustive grep, called from nowhere. What run_precision.py actually does is

grade = int(np.random.default_rng(870_000 + c).choice([1, 2, 3, 4, 5]))

— uniform. So the realised mix is 40% grade IV–V against the declared 18%, and 40% grade I–II against a declared 60%.

Grade is not cosmetic in this model. GRADE_TABLE maps grade to (bolus semi-axis fraction of R, retrograde reach fraction of the ureter): 0.10 R and 0.20 at grade I, 0.30 R and 1.00 at grade V. Semi-axis 0.10 R → 0.30 R is a 27-fold volume ratio, and reach 0.20 → 1.00 determines how many zone centroids the bolus crosses, hence how many points equation (1) has to fit. Both are monotone in grade and both make detection easier.

Which figures this biases, and how. Optimistically, all of Study 5's detection figures: per_event_sens (0.806 / 0.743 / 0.681 / 0.632 across σ for the 10 cm arm), best_sens, and never_detected (0/0/4.2/8.3%). Under the declared weights the same per-grade rates would give a lower sensitivity and a higher never-detected rate, because the easy tail is over-represented by 22 percentage points. Less obviously, specificity is affected too: bolus_path uses GRADE_TABLE[grade] to size the bolus for antegrade trials as well as refluxing ones, so healthy children also get an unrealistically large mean bolus, giving cleaner true-negative calls — so best_spec (0.833 / 0.750 / 0.792 / 0.750) is optimistic as well. low_grade_never is the exception: it conditions on grade ≤ 2 and is therefore unbiased by the mix, which is fortunate, since it is the metric the placement conclusion rests on.

The re-weighted sensitivity cannot be recovered post hoc. metrics_precision.json stores only the aggregated grid, and the per-record checkpoint metrics_precision.partial.jsonl was not retained, so the per-grade breakdown no longer exists on disk. Correcting this needs a rerun with sample_grade actually called (or per-record grades persisted and the average re-weighted), not a recomputation.

6. The base rate problem

Every headline figure in this project is computed on a cohort that is 24 refluxing and 24 healthy children — 50% prevalence, a design choice, not epidemiology. Screening performance is governed by

(2)   PPV = (Se π) / (Se π + (1 − Sp)(1 − π))

Take the most operationally realistic Study 5 cell — 10 cm at z = 0.34 with σ = 1 cm of placement error, k ≥ 4 of 6 — where Se = 0.708 and Sp = 0.792:

Prevalence πSettingPPVNPV
50%this simulation's own cohort77.3%73.1%
30%after a first febrile UTI59.3%86.4%
1%general pediatric screening3.3%99.6%

Concretely, per 1000 children screened at π = 1%: 10 refluxers, of whom 7 are flagged and 3 missed; 990 healthy, of whom 206 are flagged. 213 children go on to confirmatory VCUG to find 7 cases, i.e.

(3)   VCUGs per true case = (Se π + (1 − Sp)(1 − π)) / (Se π) = 30.1

In a project whose entire clinical premise is avoiding catheterisation and radiation, a general-population screening deployment would order thirty catheterised studies per case found. At π = 30% the same device orders 358 VCUGs per 1000 instead of 1000, a 64% reduction, at the cost of missing 88 of 300 refluxers — a trade that is at least arguable against a reference standard that itself misses ~20% of reflux and whose graders agree only 59% of the time. The post-febrile-UTI population is the only setting these numbers support.

The corollary a device engineer should take from (2): specificity, not sensitivity, is the binding constraint. Holding Se = 0.708, reaching even a 30% PPV at π = 1% requires Sp = 98.3%, against the 79.2% measured. That is a 26-fold reduction in false-positive rate, not an increment. And the unexplained 11.1% false-retrograde floor at zero motion in Study 6 sits directly on this constraint: it caps specificity from below by a mechanism nobody in this project has yet identified.

7. Small cells, wide intervals, and a shape that should not be read

Study 5 is 2304 trials but only 48 children per cell, 24 per arm, and the low-grade subset is 14 children. Wilson 95% intervals on the numbers the placement conclusion rests on:

The two low-grade intervals abut at 21.4/21.5%: on any single cell the difference is at the very edge of conventional significance. What carries the finding is that the gap persists in all four σ cells and in both metrics. That is not four independent replications, however. The seeds make it a paired design: rng(870_000 + c) fixes each child's grade and c % 2 fixes refluxer status, both independent of span and σ, and rng(880_000 + child).normal(0, sigma) draws the misplacement from the same standard normal scaled by σ. So child c has the same grade, the same diagnosis and the same normalised misplacement in every cell of the grid. Pairing is a genuine variance reduction for the 10-vs-16 cm contrast — it removes between-child grade variance from the comparison — but it also means the four σ cells share their entire child-level randomness, so the non-monotonicity of the 16 cm arm (25 → 33 → 12 → 17% never-detected) is one realisation of the same 24 children re-scored, not four independent measurements of a trend.

Accordingly the defensible claim is the persistent gap between arms, not the shape of either curve in σ, and certainly not any interpolation such as "there is an optimum near σ = 1 cm." Resolving the shape needs independent child draws per σ and n on the order of 200 per cell; at the current per-trial cost that is a scheduling decision, not a research problem.

Standing limitations that no run addresses

The SNR budget is assumed, not measured from hardware. The tissue values are from the literature. The geometry is a cylinder. And there is no phantom data and no patient data of any kind — every number in this report is a property of a program.

The sentence to take away: this work establishes that a directional estimator is internally consistent, mesh-independent in its decision, robust to non-rigid motion in a fail-safe way, and sensitive to strip placement in a way that overturns the project's own locked design — and it establishes none of that about a child, because the "cohort" is one body with jittered organs, the grade mix is 22 points easier than declared, and at realistic screening prevalence the measured operating point yields a 3.3% positive predictive value.
