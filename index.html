the claim that decides the product

Does observing more events actually help?

The case for beating VCUG rests entirely on event count: VCUG captures one or two forced voids and misses roughly 20% of reflux, while a passive wearable observes many natural cycles. That argument assumes events are independent. If a child's anatomy or electrode placement is what limits the measurement, every event on that child fails together and repetition buys nothing. So this study simulates 110 children with 6 events each, holding anatomy and placement fixed per child, and compares the measured result against what independence would predict.

The chosen rule is ≥3 of 6: 96% sensitivity at 98% specificity, against nuclear VCUG's 81% at 89%. Per event the detector runs 88% sensitivity at a 9% false-positive rate, so the gain really does come from repetition rather than from any single measurement being excellent.

Why sensitivity and specificity are shown separately. An earlier version of this analysis pooled refluxing and healthy children and counted any correct direction call as a hit. That is per-child accuracy, not sensitivity, and because half the cohort is healthy its chance floor is 89% at ≥2 of 6, which sits above the ~80% VCUG line. A coin-flip detector would have appeared to beat VCUG. The table above therefore reports the two quantities separately and prints the coin-flip floor beside them, so the comparison is like for like.
Do repeat events actually compound? Anatomy and electrode placement are held fixed per child, so any correlated failure shows up as a shortfall against the independence prediction. Here 0% of refluxing children were never detected in 6 events, meaning correlated failure is small in this model. That is the most model-dependent result on the page: real anatomical variation and real placement error are almost certainly larger than simulated, and a phantom study followed by a clinical pilot must measure this fraction directly before the multi-event argument can be relied on.
is it really direction?

What carries the signal

Amplitude alone is the quantity that reflux shares with bladder filling. Direction is the quantity that defines reflux. The ablation separates them: all features 0.89, direction only 0.85, amplitude only 0.82.

What this does not establish

LimitationConsequence
No physical dataNo phantom, animal or clinical measurement stands behind any number here. This is a feasibility envelope, not clinical performance.
Structured anatomyA cylinder with organs, not segmented CT. Real anatomical variation is almost certainly larger than what is simulated.
Single frequency50 kHz only. Multi-frequency discrimination is untested and could help or do nothing.
Motion model is structuredCommon-mode displacement plus breathing plus an independent contact term. Real awake-child motion may be worse and is not measured.
Aperture selection is greedyThe estimator picks one aperture by signal strength. A better rule may change the electrode-count conclusion from the previous study.
Grades I to IINot detectable by this method. Any claim must be scoped to grade ≥ III.
